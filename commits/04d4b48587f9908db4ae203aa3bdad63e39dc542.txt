Sprites (ticket 66): measure the mission's sprite list, and gate the limit
The owner's note says the screen looked right in play, so the ticket's first
job was a measurement, not a hunt. Measured: the port's sprite list at the
mission checkpoints *is* the reference's -- the same entries, in the same
order, at the same Y/X -- and the only drawn entry that differs is the
animated unit icon's tile word, one animation step apart, the class tickets
65 and 70 already record.

  * `scripts/compare_sprites.py` walks the sprite attribute table's linked
    list in both VDP dumps -- the port's `--vdp-dump-at exit` and the
    reference's capture -- and prints every entry with its table address and
    screen line/column, the per-line limit's outcome (which entries the 20/16
    limit drops, with their addresses), and the first entry the two disagree
    about, classified as *placement* (a different entry, order, Y, X or size:
    exit 1) or *graphic* (the tile word: the animation step, exit 0, because
    where a sprite sits is what the tool gates).
  * Measured with it: 26/26 entries at 7898 (the first disagreement is entry
    10, table +0x0050, same Y $0A1 X $101 size $05 link 11, tile $3E6 H-flipped
    against $3DA V-flipped) and 33/33 at 8148 (entry 17, table +0x0088, the
    same icon). The reference dumps are one emulator walk each with the
    recording's presses (ticket 65's --press route).
  * `headless --sprite-log PATH`: a per-entry summary of the walk (table base,
    the list's length, the busiest line, overflowing lines, dropped entries),
    so the question "is there a screen over the limit" could be asked without
    one dump per frame. Over the whole 9 600-entry Atreides route the list
    reaches at most 41 entries and the busiest line holds 14 of the 20 the
    hardware shows: the cartridge never reaches the limit.
  * `scripts/capture_sprite_limit.py` + `scripts/compare_sprite_limit.py`: the
    reference behaviour for the limit, taken on a crafted state *inside
    BlastEm* (the 68000 is hijacked at a VBlank entry and made to stream the
    state into V-RAM), with the identical state dump rendered by the port's
    own renderer. 21 entries on a line drop the twenty-first (entry 20, table
    +0x00A0); 17 are all drawn (H40's limit is 20, not H32's 16); an entry
    that is not on the line consumes no slot. Both pictures are BlastEm's
    pixel for pixel, 0 of 71 680, and the two states differ from each other's
    picture by 128 pixels (the `--cross` negative control).
  * `scripts/compare_sprite_stretch.py`: 30 consecutive frames (7891..7920)
    with the differing share per frame -- 0.2288 % to 0.2539 %, 164..182 of
    71 680 active pixels -- and every differing pixel inside the three
    rectangles the screen's own measurements give (the animated unit icon,
    the map unit beside the selected yard, the credit counter). Three entries
    whose interval BlastEm's presentation skips are static-stretch copies
    (7900 = 7902, 7910 = 7912, 7919 = 7921 are pixel-identical), which the
    check re-derives from the pictures.
  * `analysis/checkpoints.json`: the mission-map and in-game-hud notes record
    the sprite-list measurement; `docs/native-port.md` gains "The mission's
    sprite placement (ticket 66)".
  * tests: `tests/test_sprite_state.sh`, `tests/test_sprite_limit.sh` and
    `tests/test_sprite_stretch.sh`, wired into `tests/run_all.sh`. The
    house-selection selection-frame check keeps its exact-match threshold.

No game source changed; `build/dune.bin` differs from the dump only in the
header block, and `bash tests/run_all.sh` ends with ALL CHECKS PASSED.
