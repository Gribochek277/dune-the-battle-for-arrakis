ticket 98: PORT SETTINGS opens the game's own options screen
The port's fourth row answers $42 -- the OPTIONS row's own letter -- and that is
the whole of what it does. The loop's chain at $00D4C0 turns it into the result
RunFrontEnd already acts on, so the guest's own dispatch opens the guest's own
options screen: the yellow artwork, the rows, PRESS *START* TO EXIT. The evidence
is the comparison an overlay could never pass -- walking to PORT SETTINGS on next
and to OPTIONS on legacy draw the same 230 723 bytes of picture, and that picture
moved 62 987 of 76 800 pixels from the title screen to get there, on a screen the
port does not draw and could not.

This replaces ticket 85's overlay, and the overlay goes with it rather than being
left unreachable: the request, the screen-is-up state, the borrowed-cell snapshot,
the arrangement's hold branch and mdloop_hold_one are deleted, along with the two
tests that drove them. What the replacement still needs is the text plane the
row's own label is drawn from, and the window over a list longer than the screen.

Nothing is held on this path, which is measured rather than asserted in prose: 100
frames on the screen the port opened cost 1 185 831 guest instructions and
12 800 600 cycles. Ticket 85 documented ~75 SDL underruns per 120 held frames as
the price of a screen the game did not have; the price is gone because the machine
is not stopped. The reason it was stopped is still true -- a settings screen is
where the player presses buttons in order to bind them, and the game must not also
read them -- and ticket 94 enforces that where the game reads its pad instead of
freezing the game to keep it out.

One coupling broke on the way and is worth the sentence it costs: until now "the
port draws this row" and "this row opens a port screen" were one condition, so
deleting the second silently deleted the first and left the player back on a
three-row menu with no way to get where they had been. `port_label` and `code` are
two fields now, and the round trip comes back with the row on screen, three cycles
agreeing.

The scroll window became next/port_window.{c,h} -- arithmetic over three numbers,
so it is checked by enumerating them (3 081 checks over every list of 0..24 rows in
every window of 1..6 slots) instead of by eleven runs of the machine that could
only ever reach the cases a d-pad walks to. Ticket 92's end-to-end check was the
right first shape and the wrong permanent home; the end-to-end half comes back with
the screen that has a long list.
