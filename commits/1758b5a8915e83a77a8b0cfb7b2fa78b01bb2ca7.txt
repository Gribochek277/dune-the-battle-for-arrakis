ticket 104: document the two sprite-table layouts, and guard the design with a check that can fail
docs/game-architecture.md gains 'The two layouts of a sprite table' beside its sibling on
the glyph table, because both are facts about how the chip renders and both have already
cost a session each. It states the confusion plainly -- the table exists twice in a
program's life and the copies are not the same shape -- with the field-by-field table, the
$80 bias, X = 0 meaning 'do not display' rather than 'column zero', and the fact that the
table is a linked list so entries the chain never reaches are not drawn even while they
still hold attributes. That last part is why a stale table shows a stale sprite rather
than no sprite.

docs/next-engine.md's ownership ledger gets the cursor row's real answer: the tile,
palette and format are the cartridge's and may not be chosen; the position and the
priority bit are the port's and have to be, because the game's entry carries no priority
bit and does not need one on a title screen whose backdrop sets none either.

The test gains the guard the design needs, and it was tested: with a call inserted it
fails with exit 1, without it passes. It matches calls rather than words, because the
source's comments name both routines on purpose to record why they are not called, and a
check that punishes its own documentation gets edited away inside a week.

The address log could not enforce this -- it is a set over the whole run, and the game
calls both routines itself on the title screen, so they appear regardless.
