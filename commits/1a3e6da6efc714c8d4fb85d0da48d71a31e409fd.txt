ticket 158: the interface is a layer of its own, drawn over the world
The playtest's black square and sand strip are the guest's interface, and at
the world's scale they were composited as the map's right-hand columns. The
picture is now two layers: the world region is drawn as before and the
layout's interface regions are painted over it from the guest's 320x240
picture at the interface's scale, clipped to each region's own rectangle.

* `output/scene.h` carries the layer (`interfaces`, the layer's origin and
  scale, the regions' source/destination rectangles) and
  `md_scene_use_layout()` fills it from the one list in `output/layout.h`,
  refusing regions that disagree about their scale or their composition
  transform. Fit-height keeps its picture region as one picture, so the
  reference path and the 4:3 frame are untouched.
* `output/render_scene_cpu.c` composes the world's target row and paints the
  interface's regions over it; `output/render_scene_gl.c`'s composite shader
  and `output/render_scene_vk.glsl` take the same per-pixel decision from the
  same uniforms, so the three backends cannot disagree about which layer owns
  a pixel. The Vulkan fixture found the compute shader's window plane applied
  to columns left of the guest's screen, where the CPU's window_line() skips
  them; the window is screen-space and now says so in the shader too.
* The world's source stays the guest's 320 columns (plus the wide mission's
  extra): the columns under the interface are the map the guest's plane
  fetches there, and a column scrolling out from under the interface has to
  be the map's. The interface is what is clipped, not the world's fetch.
* `app/render_vdp_dump.c` gained `--interface-scale`, `--world-scale` and
  `--no-interface` (the control: the same world, the interface's regions
  dropped) and reports the resolved interface rectangles, which is how the
  checkpoint check reads them.

The checks: `port/tests/test_scene.c` proves the interface's pixels are the
guest's picture at the interface's scale at two world scales and that the
control differs inside the regions and nowhere else; the GL and Vulkan
fixtures compare the two-layer scene with the CPU backend byte for byte;
`tests/test_mission_wide.sh` renders the mission-map state at 1920x1200 with
the interface at 2x and the world at 1x, 2x and 3x -- the four regions' checksums
are one value each, the picture differs, and the control differs exactly inside
the rectangles. At equal scales nothing moves: 16:9 is still the 4:3 picture
shifted by 214 and 21:9 the same at x 367.

Docs: docs/native-port.md's layer map page, docs/port-architecture.md,
docs/widescreen.md, docs/build-and-test.md, CONTEXT.md and D73 in
docs/decisions.md; the docs site is rebuilt.
