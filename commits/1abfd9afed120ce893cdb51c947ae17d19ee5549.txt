The port's own font: a Ukrainian atlas in a reserved block, and text fields that wrap
The cartridge's font has no lower-case Latin -- the 32 tiles its ASCII range
reserves for `a`..`z` are Cyrillic -- so a locale could only ever say capitals.
This branch gives the port a font of its own for the glyphs the cartridge cannot
draw, keeps the game's font exactly where the game put it, and makes the port's
text fields measure, wrap and refuse in characters.

* `port/font/port-font.txt` is the atlas a human reviews: 92 glyphs of eight
  lines of eight pixels with the provenance line under the format tag. It is the
  glyphs the game's font lacks -- lower-case Latin, the Cyrillic lower-case
  alphabet, `Ё Є І Ї Ґ є і ї ґ`, Polish's diacritics in both cases, German's
  umlauts and `ß` -- drawn for this repository in the cartridge font's style.
  `scripts/make_port_font.py` generates `port/next/font_atlas.c` from it and
  `--check` fails the gate when the two disagree.
* `next/font.{c,h}` owns the rule: the game's own tile for a character its font
  has, the atlas's for one it does not, `?` -- reported once -- for one neither
  has. The atlas uploads to V-RAM `$7000-$7FFF` (tiles `$380-$3FF`), the block
  the ticket measured as free on all four text screens by writing a pattern into
  every candidate page and rendering it; the game's font at `$9000` is never
  written and is compared with the ROM byte for byte.
* `next/utf8.{c,h}` is the decoder: lengths, malformed bytes, and a cut at a
  character that never splits a sequence. `next/text.{c,h}` is the field: cells,
  lines, centring and the binding vocabulary's fold, a greedy wrap at spaces, a
  word cut at a one-line field with one report, and never a cell outside the
  field.
* `next/guest_text.c` keeps ticket 124's one-instruction-per-step shape; a
  translated UTF-8 word differs only where a character becomes a tile (the glyph
  lookup and the masks that OR the game's `$480` base in), and the atlas is
  uploaded at the routine's entry, never between two cells. A word wider than the
  rest of its line is refused with a report and the guest's bytes drawn.
* The port's screens draw through the text layer (`port_screen.c`), the settings
  title wraps onto the line under it, and the mapping screen raises only the
  binding names it inserts into a sentence -- the vocabulary is not translated.
* `port/locale.ini` gains the `[uk]` section: every key, the words a Ukrainian
  player reads, `mapping.title`'s nineteen cells wrapping onto the second line.
* The English screens are byte-identical: the title, options, settings and
  mapping VDP dumps are unchanged, and no atlas tile appears on an English screen
  (the no-lower-case-Cyrillic property extended to the new font).

Checks: `port/tests/test_utf8.c` and `port/tests/test_font.c` in
`make -C port test`; `tests/test_port_font.sh` (slow tier) compares `[uk]` cells
against `port/locale.ini` through the ROM's `$059DB6` glyph table and the atlas
source, checks `$7000`/`$9000`, the English property, a missing glyph, a wrap, a
cut and a refused guest label, and writes the screenshots under
`analysis/screenshots/`. Docs: decisions D47, `next-engine.md`, `locale.md`,
`game-architecture.md`, `port-architecture.md`, `CONTEXT.md`, the
reverse-engineering notes' measurements, and the rebuilt `docs/html`.
