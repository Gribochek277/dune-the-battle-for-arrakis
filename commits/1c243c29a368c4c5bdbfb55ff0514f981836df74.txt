ticket 101: a ledger of who owns which byte of the port's settings screen
The documentation criterion asked for 'which parts are the guest's data and which are
the port's code', and the honest answer needed a table, because the question a reader
actually has is 'may I change this?' -- so every row carries that answer as a column.

Nine rows: the backdrop, the glyphs, the palette, the priority bits, the text cells,
the rows, a host list's content, the cursor, the way out. Each names where it comes
from -- resource index $0E through LoadScreenResource, the font at $057DB6, 128 bytes
from $02A82E, plane B from $C000 -- and the two 'no' rows that are not obvious each
say what happened when someone tried: 48 palette bytes instead of 128 makes text black
because the glyph's colour lives past 48, and clearing a priority bit means reading
plane A, which is not in the 68000 address space, so the read returns ROM and the write
puts ROM into the name table.

Two rules come out of it and they are the design: reuse an asset's index, never another
screen's identity -- which is why the artwork is byte-exact for free and why the test
can compare against the game's own screen instead of a snapshot the port made of
itself; and own the smallest region that works, because every defect this screen ever
produced came from reaching outside it.
