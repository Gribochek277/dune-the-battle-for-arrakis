port: the SN76489 PSG, its samples and the WAV they go into (ticket 58)
The port had no sound: the Z80's and the 68000's register writes to the two
sound chips were recorded as ordered events and nothing was synthesised. This
is the first half of that -- the PSG -- and the machine's whole audio path
around it.

port/runtime/psg.c is the chip: the latch/data byte protocol of the SN76489
(including the volume and noise data bytes the smspower page says are not
ignored, which BlastEm drops), three tone channels, the noise generator (a
16-bit LFSR, so periodic noise repeats every 16 shifts -- the Sega parts -- and
white noise feeds the parity of bits 0 and 3 into bit 15) with its four shift
rates, the documented 2 dB attenuator, and the counters behind all of it. Its
clock is the same one BlastEm uses: a tick every 240 master clocks,
223721.5625 Hz, from which the documented frequency arithmetic follows
(f = Tick/(2N) = InputClock/(32N) with the 3579545 Hz input clock).

port/runtime/sound.c is what turns that into audio: the chip instance both CPUs
write to, the machine's cycle counter as its time base (md68k_step advances it
after every instruction, so a register write lands at the cycle the guest made
it at), and a box-filtered resampler at 44 100 Hz mono whose sample grid is
exact -- one sample is 715909/588 master clocks, and a run of N cycles makes
exactly floor(N * 7 * 588 / 715909) samples. port/runtime/wav.c writes them as a
16-bit mono WAV without an audio library, the way png.c writes a picture.

Both write paths reach the chip: the Z80's $7F11 (mdz80.c) and the 68000's own
$C00011 with its odd mirrors, a new MD68K_REGION_PSG block in md68k_mem.c whose
even bytes carry nothing, as the hardware's data bus does. The PSG is not reset
by the Z80's reset line: BlastEm resets the YM2612 there and the chip's
registers are the guest's to keep.
