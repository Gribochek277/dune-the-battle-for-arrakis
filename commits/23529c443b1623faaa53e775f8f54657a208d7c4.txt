ticket 104: the one routine the port cannot call is the one that relocates its own stack
Searched instead of reasoning: the move.l a7,usp / move.l usp,a7 pair appears exactly
twice in the whole ROM, both inside BuildSpriteTable. No other routine in the cartridge
does it. The port calls ten guest routines through the same guest_call and all ten return
-- ClearBackdrop, DisableVdpDisplay, FlushDmaQueue, LoadScreenResource, QueueDmaTransfer,
SetDisplayWidth, SetPlaneSize, SetWindow, WaitVBlank -- and none of them touches USP.

Stated carefully: this is a correlation, not a proven mechanism, and six mechanisms on
this ticket have already died. But it is the first fact that distinguishes the failing
case from ten working ones, which is a different kind of evidence and worth naming as
what it is. It also hints at what the routine is for -- relocating the stack, disabling
interrupts and pushing twelve deep is what code does when it cannot trust the stack it
was called on.

The pragmatic path is now unavoidable and is written down with its cost: stop calling the
routine, write the hardware's own eight-byte sprite entry directly (the port's V-RAM copy
is proven, the cursor pattern address $00D5EA is measured, the format is the chip's
manual). What that loses is the 'moved by the game's own code' half of the criterion's
wording, and that is said out loud rather than glossed -- the sprite would be the game's
data, in the game's format, at the game's address, placed by the port. Met in substance,
not in letter.

Second question for the owner on this ticket, alongside the attribute and column
positions.
