186: the mission map view is one module of whole routines, and it goes solo
The port's largest transcription is a view module: `port/next/mission_map_view.c`
and its five parts now hold seventeen rows as the guest's routines written as C
-- the view record and its scroll, the viewport descriptor loader, the plane's
rows and their packing and upload, the draw script, the object and edge
repaints, and the cursor and overlay records -- and each routine is executed
from its entry to its `rts` in C, charging every guest instruction at its own
address. A dispatch ends at exactly two kinds of boundary, and neither is "one
instruction":

* **a call out** -- to a row of the group (`LoadViewportDescriptor`,
  `QueueDmaTransfer`), to another group's row (`GetStructureByIndex`,
  `GetUnitByIndex`) or to code that is still the guest's (`AddSpriteRecord`).
  The C pushes the return address the guest's `jsr` pushed, leaves pc at the
  callee and carries on where the callee's `rts` brings the machine back; or
* **a VDP data-port write** -- `UploadMapRow`'s eight long writes of a plane
  row. The FIFO's service time is the clock at the write and the machine
  advances the clock between dispatches, so each write opens its own.

Everything else is one dispatch: a whole row of cells, a whole pack, a whole
frame step. `mission_map_view.h` states the frame the ticket asked for: what
changes the scroll, what uploads, what repaints and in what order, and what the
panels ask the view to draw (nothing of their own -- the map shows their
structures through the same object pass).

The group is `mission-map-view`, **solo** with entry `seam` (D102) and
**complete**: zero delegations, which the mechanism now enforces for it. The
first draft was exact in every dimension except one interrupt frame -- 84 bytes
of stale stack, 6 cycles of wait-loop overshoot and one pixel at the frame
boundary -- and the cause was a real defect (a data write dispatched at its
entry's clock instead of the guest's), found by adding the per-row fixture this
commit keeps: a constructed state run through the guest's code and the C, every
register, the 64 KiB, the VDP write counters and the cycles compared. With the
fix the Atreides mission pair is identical in picture, work RAM, VDP write
counters and DMA counts, the Z80's state, the YM2612 timeline and the clock,
and the translation is smaller by exactly the 131 692 instructions the C reports.

The fixture set is the group's own, because the state corpus's span shape is one
guest instruction per dispatch (the transcription this deletes): two route states
hashed at their frames in `analysis/fixtures/mission-map-view/` (inside the
held-right stretch and at the build panel's checkpoint), the three constructed
situations the route cannot show (a side change, a structure placed, a unit
destroyed) and the whole-recording pair. The rows are declared unmeasured in
`tests/test_oracle.sh` with that reason, and their old per-instruction fixtures
are deleted.

The two fidelity differences the notes carried now have owners. A unit's icon is
its facing: `sub_00B3B2` reads the unit record's `+$6A`, which the aim/order
path owns -- the view owns the node and the plane, not the angle. The build
panel's bar is a countdown: `StructurePanelFrame` ages `$FFFFC574` by the
`FrameCounter` delta and `seg_code_00416E` draws the bar from `$FFFFC768`, so
"the port's drains, the reference's is static" is two values of that countdown.
D102 records both, and `docs/reverse-engineering-notes.md` has the measurements.

The dashboard gains a third row mode, `chunked` (a dispatch that answers several
guest instructions), and the mechanism, the inventory and the engine-equivalence
check now read a span row's reported instruction count instead of assuming one
per dispatch.

Proof: `tests/test_mission_map_view.sh` (the two banked states, the 8 000-frame
pair, the checkpoints 7 898 and 8 148, `--no-replace` byte for byte against
legacy), `make -C port test` (the constructed fixtures), `tests/test_oracle.sh`,
`tests/test_stages.py`, `tests/test_flag_groups.sh`, `tests/test_docs.sh` and
`bash tests/run_all.sh` (fast tier, ALL CHECKS PASSED).
