next: the mission is wider than 320, the HUD on its edges, the band's units appended
Ticket 132 makes the mission's picture wider than the guest's 320 pixels
without stretching the interface, on ticket 120's layout and ticket 134's
measured layer map. The decision is D61; the numbers are in
docs/native-port.md, "The wide mission world", and docs/widescreen.md.

The picture is the guest's, shifted: the wide layout's world region has the
source {-extra, 0, 320+extra, 240} at the interface's integer scale, its right
edge on the target's right edge. So the guest's screen is the picture's right
part and every interface anchor ticket 134 measured keeps its pixels -- the
credits' 16 from the right, the icon column's 16, the radar's 16 from the right
and the bottom -- with no region of its own; the cursor and the selection box
are placed by the world region's transform, which is what "screen coordinates"
means. The scene gained one number, source_x, which the CPU, GL and Vulkan
backends all read; the fit-height draws pass 0, so the frame matrix and every
fidelity picture are byte-identical.

The extra is target_width / interface_scale - 320: 0 at 4:3 (the mission keeps
the fit-height layout, exactly the cartridge's view), 107 at 16:9 and 480 high
(427x240, filling the target), and capped at 127 by the VDP's sprite X field (a
sprite's screen x is -128..383 and the raw value 0 is the hardware's "do not
display"), so 21:9 is capped at 447 guest columns with a 226-pixel bar on the
left, where the world would have grown. scripts/mission_wide.py generates
port/next/mission_wide_table.c from analysis/mission-layers.json -- each
class's role, anchor, measured rectangle, palettes, priority and tile range --
and --check fails when the two disagree, so the layer map is the
implementation's source.

The guest's data moves deliberately and only two bounds: the map view's left
limit becomes its base plus the extra (the map's left edge is the picture's,
not the screen's) and the cursor's becomes 24 minus the extra (it reaches the
band), with the guest's bases remembered, relearned when the guest rewrites
them, and restored at 0 extra. The placement handler's hardcoded 24/296 in the
mission's C read the same accessors, with the reason written next to the cases;
at 0 extra they are the guest's own values.

The band's units are appended, not a builder takeover: BuildSpriteTable culls a
part left of its 320-pixel screen, so next_mission_wide_append_sprites() writes
those parts after the guest's build (at $003E60 and in the first handler's tail
row $003644, both the port's C) with the guest's own encoding, for the nodes
whose position record is a unit's (the layer map's rule), without stepping the
animation and without charging the guest's clock. It is idempotent within a
frame because the handler can run more than once while only one path builds.

The shell adopts the mission's layout for the same target
(md_shell_use_layout), app/wide_mission.c composes it from the layer map, and
the front end switches on next_mission_wide_live() -- the pool's unit record
and the window plane's registers -- so the full-screen panels stay fit-height.
vdp-render gained --target and --wide so a checkpoint's state renders at the
three layouts deterministically; tests/test_mission_wide.sh compares those
pictures (4:3 has no wide layout; 16:9 is the 4:3 picture shifted by 214 with
the world's band on its left and the credit counter's region at the picture's
right edge; 21:9 caps with a black bar; the build panel returns to fit-height),
the guest's clock and work RAM (the 4:3 run's apart from where the wide view
writes), and a held right scroll that puts a unit in the band, appended. The
fast fixtures (port/build/mission-wide-test, 184 checks) prove the table, the
layout and the guest data, and port/tests/test_scene.c's wide fixture proves
the scene's source offset: the band is the plane's columns left of the screen
and a sprite at screen x -20 is drawn in it.
