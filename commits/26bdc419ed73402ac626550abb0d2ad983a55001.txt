160: the mission's picture has one scale, and its own rectangles are drawn once
The owner's third playtest report of the mission screen: with ticket 159's
geometry (a fullscreen 1920x1200 target, display.ini asking for the interface at
2x and the world row at 1x, which resolves to 5x) the credits counter and the
guest's radar were on screen twice -- the world layer's copy of the guest's own
interface columns at 5x and the interface layer's copy at 2x -- and the
selection rectangle was drawn on a black bar while the units it marked were on
the map. The owner sent a reference picture of the composition it should have:
one copy of each, at the world's scale, and named the .ini beside the binary as
the reproduction.

The mission's picture has one scale, and it is the world's
----------------------------------------------------------
`mda_wide_mission_layout()` composes the guest's 320x240 at the *resolved world
scale* instead of the interface's, so the composition's transform is
`composition.origin + guest * world_scale` for the map, the interface's regions
and the port's own overlays alike. An interface region's destination is then
exactly the world's transform of its source: the layer repaints the pixels the
world already drew, and the picture has one copy of each. At equal scales
nothing moved (the two copies coincided, which is why the doubling never showed
before ticket 159). The interface's own scale keeps its meaning where a screen
is all interface -- the panels, the menus, OPTIONS, the title, the house
selection are fit-height at the INTERFACE SCALE row.

The world is clipped by the interface's rectangles
-------------------------------------------------
`md_scene_guest_pixel_is_interface()` (output/scene.h) answers whether a guest
pixel is one an interface region is drawn from, and the world layer's sprite
pass drops it (the planes underneath stay: the map under the HUD is the map, and
a column that scrolls out from under the interface stays the map's -- D73's
reason). All three backends take the same decision from the same scene: the CPU
row, the GL composite shader and the Vulkan compute shader. With one scale the
clip and the repaint coincide and no pixel moves today; it is what makes "one
copy" a rule rather than two placements happening to agree.

The port's own rectangles go through the same function
-----------------------------------------------------
`md_layout_guest_to_target()` / `md_layout_guest_from_target()` /
`md_layout_guest_scale()` (output/layout.h) are the guest pixel's target pixel
and back through the region the picture is drawn through -- the world's in the
wide mission, the picture's in fit-height, the identity for a NULL layout. The
pointer, the overlay and the backends call them; the overlay's shapes are the
caller's value (`mda_selection_overlay`, filled by app/play_backend.c from the
gesture and the set) and app/selection_overlay.c only draws them, with the
markers' arms a guest length times the transform's scale. `vdp-render` gained
`--overlay X0,Y0,X1,Y1`, `--marker X,Y`, `--badge N` and `--no-overlay`, so a
placement is a picture and not a screenshot.

What proves it
--------------
* tests/test_mission_overlay.sh (new, fast tier): the same mission dump with the
  overlay and with `--no-overlay` at the owner's 1920x1200, 854x480 at 2x,
  1300x600 at 3x and 4x, 1920x1200 at 5x/5x and the cartridge's 4:3 -- every
  differing pixel must be on exactly one rectangle's stroke at
  `world.dest + (guest - world.source) * world_scale`, inside the picture, with
  the selection's colour on all four sides and none of it in the control;
* tests/test_mission_wide.sh: the interface-on picture is byte-identical to the
  `--no-interface` control at every one of those cases, and every region is the
  picture's transform of its guest rectangle (before, the control differed --
  that *was* the doubling);
* port/tests/test_layout.c: the transform's round trip, a bar refusing, the
  scale; port/tests/test_mission_wide.c and test_display_layout.c: the layout's
  numbers; the GL and Vulkan fixtures compare all three backends' pictures with
  the rule in place.
