175: a subsystem is measured, paired, then solo, and parity keeps the guest's cycles
The rule every slice of the porting epic (tickets 172-201) is costed against,
and the half the project had never written: what a subsystem's contract is once
the guest's code is no longer beside it (D91).

While both forms exist the ported one is indistinguishable from the
transliterated guest -- cycle cost included -- because the cycle cost is what
the frame boundary, the game's timers, the pad's 56 000-cycle handshake and the
sound driver's polling are built on. Two of this project's own defects are that
sentence: D37's missing DRAM refresh put the VBlank at a different point in the
guest's frame and moved a unit's turn ten frames off, and the house selection's
one-cycle replacement stepping leaves the Z80's accounting counters up to six
instructions apart. Relaxing the cycle bound is how a port silently stops being
the game.

A stage belongs to a group, never to a routine. **Measured**: only the guest
runs and the measurement exists. **Paired**: both run and the differential
comparison is the proof -- work RAM byte for byte, the picture pixel for pixel,
the chip writes, the 68000 cycles -- and a *complete* group is one whose C hands
no address back (complete is measured, not declared). **Solo**: only the port's
C runs; the contract is the banked fixtures of ticket 173 plus the playtest
route of ticket 177, and the guest's instruction path, its scratch registers and
the frame's total cycle budget may stop being equal while the fixtures' entries
still are. Retiring the cycle model is not a loss of a check, because the
fixtures carry the guest's entries, RAM windows, chip writes and cycle points
forward.

Solo is earned, not declared, and all three conditions are required: zero
delegations in the group (the inventory ticket 174 prints), the group's fixtures
committed, and the playtest route green. `tests/test_stages.py` refuses a claim
over a module that can hand an address back, requires the fixture directory and
the recording to exist, and prints the reason; its negative controls provoke
every failure it can see plus one legal solo claim, so the branch the project
has not entered yet is exercised rather than assumed.

The classification is data, not prose. `analysis/porting-stages.json` records
all 30 rows of the replacement table in eight groups: two complete (the pad
protocol; the guest's text), six partial with what each C hands back named (the
options and title screens, the house selection, the mission cursor, the map
view's sixteen rows, and the two sidebar panels that answer two instructions of
each and are the port's C but not the port's design). No group is solo yet. The
port's own features -- the port screens, the pointer, the renderer and the
widescreen shell, presentation time, the locale and the font -- are listed apart
because there is no guest form for them to agree with. The record says which
part is which and why a transcription that is never replaced stays evidence.

The **porting dashboard** (`docs/html/dashboard/index.html`) is generated from
that file by the same `scripts/porting_stages.py` the fast-tier check runs, so
the page cannot say what the check does not see; D91's own classification table
is compared with it and with the replacement table. `legacy` is untouched (D32)
and the fidelity checkpoints keep their meaning for a structural reason: they
render `legacy`, so a solo group in `next` cannot move a pixel of them. The
engine equivalence check and the group's own differential check are edited with
a control build when a solo group deliberately diverges, never deleted
(AGENTS.md). The migration recipe now walks the three stages instead of five
steps -- and still sends a ported slice through ticket 172's state layer --
