next: the edge scroll's band is the device's, and the pointer's is the picture's edge
The mission's edge scroll starts when the view's position leaves a dead zone, and
the guest's dead zone is a d-pad's: 80 by 60 pixels around the picture's centre.
The pad never moves the cursor directly -- it moves the view, and the cursor rides
on it -- so that band is what keeps a held direction from fighting the view's own
step. A mouse moves the cursor directly (ticket 127 writes the pixel into
$FFFFBF12/$FFFFBF14), so the same band made a hand that only meant to point at
the right third of the picture scroll the view by itself.

Ticket 143 gives the band to the device that drove the frame. A pointer's band is
one twentieth of the picture at its own edge -- 16 of 320 pixels across, 11 of 224
down -- because the picture, not the window, is what the pointer has already been
converted to: a scaled window needs no second rule and a wider frame (tickets
130-132) keeps the band at its edge in the same proportion. The pad's and the
keyboard's band is the guest's, instruction for instruction. The device is a
latch read once where the handler reads the pad ($003118/$003AD0): the pointer
wins the frame it moved or clicked, a held direction wins a frame the pointer left
alone, and a frame neither touched keeps the last device -- one rule in one place,
so a mixed frame does not depend on which check runs first, and a click that
arrives without motion does not jump the band back to the guest's. A corner is
both bands at once: the two axes are tested independently, as the guest tests
them, so the view moves diagonally.

The ticket's premise was checked and corrected. It names the two constants in
UpdateMissionCursor's test ($00345E's 120, $003468's 80), but the Atreides route
never runs that handler: the map is in placement mode (ticket 126), and
UpdatePlacementCursor hardcodes the same rectangle ($003CE2/$003CE8,
$003D92/$003D98). Covering only the first handler would have changed nothing a
player sees; both are covered.

next/mission_edge_band.{c,h} owns the latch and the sixteen instructions in which
the guest's own number *is* the band: the four comparisons and the four
subtractions of each handler's two tests. The rest of those tests -- the table
reads, the negations that turn an entry into an edge, the idle pair -- is the
guest's arithmetic and does not depend on the device, so it stays in the
transcribed files. One instruction per call is deliberate: it keeps
tests/test_mission_cursor.sh's transcribed comparison exactly what it was (50 244
calls, the step count identical, the translation down by 50 244, the interpreted
count and the clock identical), so the pad's path keeps the strongest evidence in
the tree rather than trading it for a weaker one. Nothing in the generated
listing or in assembly changed.

A bug fell out of the switch fixture: a click with no motion did not clear
$FFFFBF48, the pad the handler's idle case coasts in the direction of, so a click
after a held d-pad re-dispatched the idle case through the guest's coast loop at
$003210 and the pointer's direction-clearing there dropped the direction the loop
had just fed itself -- the frame spun for ever. next/mission_cursor.c now forgets
the pad's direction on a pointer frame, a move or a click alike. legacy has no
pointer, so nothing there changes.

Tests. port/tests/test_replacement.c runs the handlers frame by frame on a
machine that is still standing: the pad's band against the guest's own code from
100/109, 230/109, 160/109, 160/60, 160/200 and 300/210 (every branch of both
tests) with every register, flag, byte and cycle equal; the pointer's band at
230/109 leaving the view at 230 where the guest's band takes it to 227; a pointer
resting at 315 settling the view at exactly 304; a pointer frame inside both
bands equal to the guest's run byte for byte; and the switch -- pointer, pointer,
pad, release, click -- moving the view on the pad's frame and keeping it after
the click. tests/test_mission_edge_band.sh is the Atreides recording through the
playable window: the pointer at 230 leaves the view at 230, at 310 it takes it to
304, a d-pad held from 7 760 moves it off 230 (233) and its release settles it on
the guest's 200 (7 850), the hand's next move leaves it at its own 240 (7 960),
and a control build with the rule removed puts the same pointer back on 200.

tests/test_mission_mouse.sh is edited, not deleted: its click run pinned the
guest's settle at x 200 as "the edge scroll's", which is exactly the behaviour
this ticket removes for a pointer. It now pins the hand's own 230; the click still
reaches unit 38's cell ($8DB) and record ($FF14C8), because the cell is computed
before the band's test. The pad's own check (tests/test_mission_cursor.sh) and the
selection box and group order checks are unedited and pass.
