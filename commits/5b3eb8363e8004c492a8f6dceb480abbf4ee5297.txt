VDP dump: hold the bits the memories store, not the read-back's own
The reference capture refused to write a dump at frame 400: all 64 C-RAM
entries differed between the CPU-driven read and the event-log replay, by
exactly 0x111 each.  C-RAM stores nine bits per entry, so those five bits are
not palette: BlastEm composes them from the VDP's read buffer (vdp.c,
CRAM_READ/VSRAM_READ take `cram[...] & CRAM_BITS` with
`fifo[fifo_write].value & ~CRAM_BITS`), and a probe in the dump routine showed
the value coming back is the FIFO slot's -- the write made four writes ago.
It is the machine's write history: 0x000 at frames 10-200, 0x110 at 250-350,
0x111 at 400, 0x101 at 430, and no port can reproduce it.

capture_vdp_state.py now keeps the bits the VDP stores in the dump (C-RAM
0x0EEE per word, V-SRAM 0x07FF) and records what the read-back added under
structures.<name>.unstored_bits; the replay cross-check compares stored bits,
so a genuinely wrong dump still fails with its structure, offset and both
values, and the frame it happened at.  compare_vdp_state.py reports a dump
that carries read-back bits instead of reading it as state, and says which
bits of C-RAM and V-SRAM it compares for.  The port masks writes the same way,
so both dumps are in that convention: at frame 400 the port's V-RAM, C-RAM,
V-SRAM and 23 of 24 registers are the reference's byte for byte, which
tests/test_vdp_state.sh now checks alongside frame 10.
