The renderer is a seam: the machine hands over a state, a renderer draws it
`port/chips/vdp.c` both modelled the VDP and drew its picture, so
"replace the renderer" meant editing the chip. It now models the chip and
stops: the picture is an implementation of `port/output/renderer.h`, which
is handed the VDP's state `const` and a surface the caller owns.

* `output/renderer.h`, `output/renderer.c`: the interface
  (`md_renderer_draw(renderer, vdp, surface)`), the surface rule and the
  registry `--renderer NAME` reads. The contract a backend works from is in
  the header: who owns the surface and how long it lives, when a draw
  happens (one per delivered frame, at the VBlank entry, 128 006 cycles =
  16.69 ms = 59.92 Hz), that C-RAM is re-read on every draw and no
  palette-change signal exists, that there are no dirty regions, that a
  partial or interrupted frame never reaches a renderer, and that
  everything is single-threaded.
* `output/surface.h`: the picture (RGB24, top row first, 320x240, the
  caller's memory) as one type the renderer, the PNG writer
  (`md68k_png_write_surface()`) and the window (`mdp_window_present()`) all
  use, so those two sinks are backends of the same seam.
* `output/render_software.c`: the renderer the port has always had, moved
  out of `chips/vdp.c` with its arithmetic unchanged; the plane/sprite
  fixtures now draw through the seam and `port/build/vdp-render` too.
* `output/render_stub.c`: the second implementation -- one flat colour per
  frame and a digest of the state it was handed. It is the proof that a
  renderer is swappable rather than wrapped.
* `chips/vdp.{c,h}`: the rendering section and `md68k_vdp_render()` are
  gone, and the header now says what a renderer may read.
* `--renderer NAME` selects the implementation in both front ends (software
  by default, a typo is an error), and both create one renderer and one
  surface and hand the surface to their sink.
* `docs/port-zones.json`: the seven new `output/` modules and one direction
  the renderer needs, `output -> chips` (the reverse is deliberately absent:
  `chips/` cannot name a renderer, which is the seam).

Proof. The gate that existed before the seam says no pixel moved: the
pre-seam build (350b433) and this one rendered 168 full frames -- every
catalog checkpoint plus the moving screens around them, frames 390..9508 --
byte for byte identically, and `scripts/fidelity.sh --all` reports all seven
checkpoints inside their gates (the three pixel-exact ones at 0). The second
half is new: the boot runs through the software renderer and through the
stub and the machine state comes out identical while the pictures differ --
`port/tests/test_renderer.c` (5 fixtures inside `make -C port test`) and
`tests/test_renderer.sh` (120-frame harness runs, both engine arrangements,
`--png-stretch` through the stub, an unknown name refused, and the window's
64 KiB of work RAM identical through both renderers). Ticket 72's
equivalence check and ticket 61's pacing check are unmoved.

docs/decisions.md gains D29 (where the seam lives, why the state is `const`,
why the stub is a recorder) and the site is rebuilt in the same commit.
