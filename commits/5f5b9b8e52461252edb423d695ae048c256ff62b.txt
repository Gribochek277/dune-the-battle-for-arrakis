Every scene field is a field the picture reads: the guest rectangle's size drives the draw, and the active area is two numbers
The rule D39 states -- a scene field arrives with the drawing that reads it --
turned out not to hold for the field set itself: the CPU backend drew the frame's
320-pixel line at `guest.x` and clipped it to the target, so `guest.width` was
never read, and `active` was a rectangle whose x and width were the frame's by
construction. Two fields a backend could corrupt without the picture changing are
two fields the negative control could not see.

The guest rectangle now means both things it says: where the picture sits in the
target and how much of it is drawn (clipped to the frame's 320 and to the target,
so a narrower rectangle shows less of the picture rather than the whole one). The
active area is `active_top` and `active_height`, the two numbers the backend needs
for the backdrop rows above and below it, with the guard that keeps a scene whose
active area was set wrongly from reading the line tables past their end. Ticket
120's layout changes these numbers and not the contract; ticket 121's backend
reads the same ones.

The corruption control now covers them: 31 fields, including the guest's origin,
width and height and the active area's first line and height, each of which
changes the picture (189 checks, 0 failures). The docs carry the new field names
and counts, and docs/html is regenerated.
