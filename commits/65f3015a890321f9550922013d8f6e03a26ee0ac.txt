185: the record world is the port's C, and the guest says what a handle means
The game's data model in C: twenty-four rows in port/next/records.c and its
three parts, the `records` group, solo with entry seam (D99). The handle library
(the side record, the six-byte team test, the two pointer tables and the two
one-based cell tables, the class tags, MakeUnitHandle's live-record test,
CheckHandle's three answers, the position with its per-shape origin, the cell
index); the two side-record clearers and the colour writer; the four team-list
links with the free pool at $FFFFBEAA; AllocateStructure and CreateUnit (the
allocators' create half, with the per-type ranges, the 98- and 140-byte clears
and the counters); and IsCellExplored, the reader of the map's sticky explored
bit. Every record field is the state layer's accessor; the two bytes the notes
do not name (+$5E on a type-$19 unit, +$F on a structure) are the guest's bytes
at the guest's offsets and are recorded as blockers rather than invented.

Two shapes, chosen by measurement. Twenty-two rows are whole routines.
CreateUnit and ClearSideRecords are spans, one instruction per dispatch: with
them whole every state matched and the audio did not -- the recording has a
VBlank inside both, a whole-routine dispatch holds it off until the routine ends,
and one YM2612 register write moved one cycle (audio differed at byte 11339600,
the YM timeline at byte 18586164). With the two split, the mission route is
identical in picture, work RAM, audio, YM timeline and cycles, and the
instruction count is lower by exactly the 175 516 the C reports it stood in for.

A call inside a row is inlined with its stack -- the return address the guest's
jsr wrote and the callee's rts popped are both reproduced, so the corpus's RAM
window is the guest's. The guest's own oddities are kept because the corpus is
the guest: GetRecordPosition's first branch tests the caller's flags, MOVEQ does
not clear V/C in the translation, a move.b keeps the destination's upper 24 bits,
and the byte written through the layer goes through the bus outside work RAM
because a cartridge write is ignored there and a fault in the layer.

Proof: 23 committed fixtures (the one row no committed route enters,

is the fixture hashes, the pair, the group-off control, the house-selection route
where the group is selected and never entered, and the playtest route's
checkpoints. tests/test_oracle.sh gains the 23 measured rows, the stage record
and D91's table name the group, the inventory measures it complete with zero
