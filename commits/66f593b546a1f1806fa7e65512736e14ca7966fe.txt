Audio: record what the cartridge sounds like, and compare two recordings
The port can write its own audio as a WAV and nothing could say whether that
audio is what the cartridge makes, so "the port has no sound" and "the port's
sound is wrong" were the same sentence. This adds both halves of the
measurement and no chip: the port's 44 100 Hz output is untouched.

The reference is BlastEm's own recorder, not a rendering of its VGM log. The
`i` key (ui.record_video) calls render_save_audio(), which appends the buffer
mix_and_convert() produced -- every audio source summed after its own one-pole
lowpass and the overall gain, 16-bit stereo at 48 000 Hz, which is the byte
stream the sound device gets. The other strings the binary carries are not
audio: -y writes one WAV per YM2612 channel before the mix and without the PSG,
and the VGM log is the chips' register writes, whose renderer would have to be
a YM2612 -- the very thing a port-side comparison is measuring.
scripts/capture_audio.py drives the recording with the same GDB-stub session
the reference pictures use (presses at exact VBlank entries, the recorder's key
typed while the CPU is stopped) and writes
analysis/reference/checkpoint-<name>.wav. Two runs of it are byte-identical
(sha256 d8a4b87b for the house-selection checkpoint, twice), because the pinned
configuration syncs the emulator to audio and the toggles land at fixed
emulated positions.

scripts/compare_audio.py reads both files with Python's wave module, downmixes
and normalises them, finds the delay between the two recordings from their RMS
envelopes, and reports peak, RMS, the dominant spectral peak and the
differences per 0.25 s window, ending in a verdict that names the first window
that fails and one of six reasons -- length, time shift, silence, level, pitch,
envelope -- or a pass. It never resamples: each side's features are measured at
its own rate, which is why a 48 kHz stereo copy of a 44.1 kHz mono file still
passes.

What it says today: the house-selection reference (2640 frames, 2 125 824
frames = 44.288 s, peak -17.0 dBFS) against the port's WAV of the same run
(1 953 209 samples of zero) is FAIL (silence) -- the honest answer before the
YM2612 slice, from ticket 58's FM-only music. The same pair exists for the
mission-map checkpoint (132.5 s of in-game music). tests/test_audio_compare.sh
proves the verdicts on known-answer WAVs (identical, a real resampling,
silence, a 0.4 s shift, a different pitch, -12 dB) and the reference's two-run
identity whenever the emulator is present.
