196: the music is the port's data, and the sequencer proves it
The port replayed the Z80 driver's register stream, which proved the music
played and said nothing about what it was. The cartridge's two sound sets are
now text files under assets/music/: one per voice, song header, channel stream,
effect and drum record, plus the player's register program, frequency ladders
and volume ladder. The stream files keep the exact event bytes (the cartridge's
length encoding has choices) with the decoded event in the comment; --check
re-encodes every file, rebuilds every offset table and song header, regenerates
the manifest and requires a fresh extraction to be the committed tree.
--apply puts the files' bytes back and is how a changed note or instrument
reaches an image.

The timeline is the other half. tools/music/sequencer.c is the port's own code:
it reads the files and the driver's tables and follows the driver's tick
arithmetic, note/voice path and total-level scaling. For the committed
house-selection recording's 300 frames it derives all 500 YM2612 writes in the
reference's order and frame grid (compare_ym_logs.py: 0.49-frame spread, no
drift), and tools/music/ymrender.c renders that stream through the port's own
YM2612: compare_audio.py passes at the gate's 3 dB / 50 cents / 0.6-envelope
thresholds (r +0.822). The play situation is a scenario
(analysis/music/house-selection-300.scn: two starts of mission song 0 channel 0
at frames 234 and 249 at volumes $19 and $27); the notes, voice and timing are
the extracted data.

The MIDI export writes the melody, part layout and driver timing, names the FM
voice in a text event and emits no program changes: the console's four-operator
voices are not General MIDI, and D99 records that limit rather than implying
parity. The test edits a note and an instrument and proves the image changes at
exactly the two bytes those edits name, so a musical change is a data edit.

The fast check is 3 s, the slow check 19 s; the slow one needs the pinned
emulator and skips without it. docs/formats-music.md has the tree, the formats,
the sequencer and what it does not cover yet (kit/PSG paths, effect-record
fields, $69-$71).
