tickets 100-101: the port's settings screen is a screen the port owns
The owner found the flaw by looking at the screen: OPTIONS appears, then becomes
PORT SETTINGS. That is ticket 99 entering the game's options screen and painting
over its title, which is ticket 85's overlay with better manners -- the port owns
no screen, it edits one, and the player can watch it happen.

What makes the honest route cheap was measured before writing it down:
LoadScreenResource $022530 takes a resource index, and the options screen asks for
$000E. The yellow artwork is therefore an asset in a table at $02243A and not part
of the options screen's identity, so a screen of the port's own can load the same
backdrop and have never drawn anything else. Reusing an asset is not patching a
screen, and "no frame ever held the cells spelling OPTIONS" is a property a
screenshot cannot fake, which is precisely what ticket 99 could not claim.

The port has to own RunFrontEnd $00518E to name a screen the cartridge does not
have -- it is not in the replacement table, which is why the cheap route was taken
and why it was wrong. Replacing a front-end routine in C is the move tickets 84 and
90 already made twice.

100 is the front end reaching a screen the port owns, with no rows on it yet; 101
is the rows and the game's cursor; 93 re-points at 101. Ticket 99 keeps its
measurements and gains a note saying which part of it is being deleted.
