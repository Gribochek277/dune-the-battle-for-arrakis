wide mission: the HUD is its frame, so the bars are in neither layer no more (2)
The owner's playtest: order a structure and the progress bar under the build
strip is missing -- and with the default (GPU) presenter, which is how it looked
like a `--renderer vk` bug. It is not: `--renderer software`, `scene` and `vk`
draw the bar's frame byte for byte, and `--presenter gpu` matches `surface`
exactly. The bar is lost in the composition.

The bar is VDP sprite 5 of the sidebar block, tile $7B2, at picture (272,112).
The layer map's measured tile ranges are credits $7B6-$7B8, structure-icon
$794-$7A6, radar $680-$6B0 and the rest; $7B2 is none of them -- no checkpoint
had a build in progress, so the bar was on none of their screens. The class test
therefore called the sprite the world's, and the world layer draws the map out of
the game's state, not the VDP's sprite table: nothing drew it. Its 4:3 picture has
it because that path draws the whole VDP frame.

D156's own words are the fix: "the HUD has a frame of its own". The frame is now
an interface region -- the picture's 320x240 at the HUD's scale, held right -- so
the layer's pass covers it, and the VDP's sprite list is judged by priority
inside that frame (`md_scene_sprite_in_interface_frame()`), whatever tile each
sprite names. The measured classes keep their rectangles, tiles and anchors; the
frame classifies nothing (its tile range is empty on purpose) and a layout that
declares no frame is the union of its regions, so every fixture's picture is
what it was.

Also fixed by the same rule, and seen in the same frame: the cursor bracket's top
line, which the class's rectangle cut by one picture row.

The checks follow the rule instead of the class list: `compare_band.py` and
`tests/test_mission_wide.sh` skip the HUD's *sprites* at the frame's scale, and
`vdp-render` prints the frame's region on its own line (`layout interface
frame:`) so a reader cannot mistake the pass for a measured class.
`port/tests/test_scene.c` carries the bar's case as a fixture: a priority sprite
whose tile is no class's is the frame's, a priority sprite outside the frame is
the world's, and a layout with no frame keeps its union.
