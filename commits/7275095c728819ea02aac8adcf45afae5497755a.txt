167: a key's press edge is latched, so a tap shorter than a poll is a press
The playtest report was "in the mission it is impossible to switch scaling with
F5/F6". Measured with real keys at the mission's wide layout: four `xdotool key
F5` presses three seconds apart, of which two changed the picture (auto -> 5x,
5x -> 6x). Ticket 165's cycle was right -- every press that arrived did change
the picture -- so the presses were being lost before the app, and the log said
nothing about them either.

The cause was in port/platform/keyboard.c and nowhere else:
mdp_keyboard_poll() sampled SDL_GetKeyboardState() once per poll and derived the
edge as `now & ~mdp_keyboard_down`. SDL's array is updated by the event pump, so
a tap whose key-down and key-up both arrive between two polls -- exactly what
`xdotool key` produces -- is already released when the sample is taken: no held
bit, no edge, nothing. The window's own F11 and F12 never lost a press because
they are read from the SDL event stream, not from the sampled state.

The fix is the latch the gamepad already had, applied where the keyboard's
events already are:

  * mdp_window_poll() reports every non-repeating SDL_KEYDOWN to
    mdp_keyboard_press(scancode); a scancode the backend's table does not hold
    presses nothing, so F11, F12 and every unbound key stay inert and the table
    remains the only place a scancode becomes a control.
  * mdp_keyboard_edges(sampled, &down) is the poll's whole arithmetic -- the
    sample's new bits united with the latched presses, the latch cleared as it is
    handed out -- and mdp_keyboard_poll() is that plus the sample.
  * `held` is still the sampled state and nothing else: a tap is pressed and
    never held, a key held across frames has one edge at its start, and a repeat
    (event.key.repeat, the X server's auto-repeat) is not a press.

One edge per control per poll, because a poll is a frame and the pad a frame
carries one press; two different keys in one frame are two edges.

What proves it:

  * port/tests/test_keyboard.c (port/build/keyboard-test, also in `make -C port
    test` where SDL2 is installed): every row of the backend's own table tapped
    between two polls is one press of that row's control and no level; a held key
    has one edge at its start and no second; a released key leaves neither edge
    nor level; two keys in one poll are two edges while two presses of one
    control are one; Start's Return and Space are one edge; an unbound scancode
    presses nothing; a key is one row and every control has a key. The pre-167
    sample-only arithmetic is kept as the control and loses all 22 taps. 617
    checks, no display and no SDL state.
  * tests/test_keyboard_taps.py (slow tier, ~90 s): the Atreides mission saved at
    entry 7 800, the window on a private X display at 1708x960 (the ticket's
    target, where the world row's pictures are AUTO's wide 4x, 5x's 21 extra
    columns and 6x's 4:3 view) and four real `xdotool key F5` taps that rebuild
    the target four times, each picture different from the one before; then F5
    held for a second, which rebuilds it once; then the same four taps on a
    control build with the latch left out of the poll's edge, which loses two or
    three of them -- the playtest's measurement reproduced from real keys, and
    the proof that the check can fail.
