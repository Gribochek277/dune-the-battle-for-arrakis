ticket 99: the screen knows which row opened it, and says so in its title
Committed with its defect named, because the owner found it by looking at the
screen: the title reads OPTIONS first and PORT SETTINGS a moment later. That
flicker is not a polish item. It is the design showing. What this ticket built is
the game's options screen with the port's word painted over the game's, at the
last instruction of the game's setup -- which is ticket 85's overlay wearing the
game's own clothes, and it is the opposite of what ticket 98 was for.

What is worth keeping from it is the fact and where the fact lives: the two
arrivals answer the same letter, so the difference cannot be the code, and it is
not in work RAM either, because work RAM is what the differential harness
compares. One value in the port's own memory, written from the row that was
chosen. Ticket 100 needs exactly that, and needs it for a different reason: to
pick a different screen rather than to relabel the same one.

The measurements survive too. The title is sixteen name-table cells at $A114 with
attribute $E480 | the character -- palette 7 and the priority bit, not the menu
rows' palette 3 -- and the game centres its word in the field. Writing the
nineteen characters the resource holds leaves three of them overwritten by a later
write of the game's own, which is how a title grew `%T]` on the end of it for one
evening.
