162: the mission's two mouse buttons are the pad's A and B, contextually
The right button is the pad's A in every place the mission reads a button
(unchanged from ticket 153), and the left button's release in place is the pad's
B -- the mission's own cancel -- while a unit selection stands, and the pad's A
(the select, at the pointer's own target) with nothing selected. A press that
moves past the four-pixel threshold is the selection box and its release is
never a button. Box selection and unit selection are unchanged.

Where it lives:
  * next/mission_mouse.c: one function, release_select(), is the whole context
    rule (press held back, threshold, release); the frame's step 5 calls it.
  * next/mission_pointer.{c,h}: the frame's face button is a mask
    (NEXT_MISSION_POINTER_CLICK / _CANCEL) so the one-frame seam did not grow a
    parameter; take_cancel() sits beside take_click().
  * The B is injected at the same instruction the A is -- the two cursor
    handlers' pad reads ($003118/$003AD0) through the same mask seam -- so the
    guest's own $00364A posts $42 and its own command pass cancels. It never
    becomes a pad byte; a recording has no line for it.
  * next/mission_selection.{c,h}: next_mission_selection_standing() (the port's
    set or the guest's SelectedRecord -- a structure alone is not a unit
    selection and does not make a release a cancel) and
    next_mission_selection_cancel() (empties the port's set and `$FFFFC25C`; a
    guest-only unit selection is left to the guest's own B).
  * The map keeps three rows and both function names: POINTER_CLICK is still
    the left button's function and POINTER_ORDER the right's, and the profile
    decides what the click means there. A second function number would put a
    word on the mapping screen's row that is false on every other screen.
    D80 records that and the rest of the decision.
