port: the captured control stays out of the pad until it is released
Measured with the virtual pad on the real window loop: one frame after a
capture the guest's pad recording showed `a hold 1` -- the A the player pressed
as an edit became a game input carrying its new function -- and the screen,
which tracks edges, read it as a fresh press and silently armed a second capture
with nothing pressed. That is how a player makes two swaps without seeing one.

The app now remembers what a capture consumed and withholds it from the machine
until the device reports it released (captured_held in app/app.c). The gate's
binding-flow test walks the owner's file, captures a held control, and asserts
the guest saw no `up`, the line stayed on `SWAPPED WITH PAD_Z`, and the next
ordinary press was delivered; it fails when the latch is removed.

Also measured while walking the owner's map: A on the BACK row did nothing when
it arrived as the A function (their `start = pad_a`), because the capture branch
ate the press even when the front end refused the row. It now falls through to
the choose/leave step, and tests/test_port_settings_screen.sh asserts that A on
BACK leaves the list.
