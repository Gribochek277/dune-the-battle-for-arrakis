183: the VDP library is the port's C, one dispatch per guest instruction
The routines the whole game calls instead of touching a VDP port are
port/next/vdp_library*.c now: twenty-nine rows, one per guest entry --
the register file and its shadows (the plane, text, hscroll and sprite
bases, the display pair, the window map's base and position, the fade
delay), the address arithmetic, the VRAM plane write and the CRAM clear,
the palette upload and its range clear, the sprite table's build and DMA
upload, the DMA queue's append, drain and flush, and the RLE decoder with
the glyph, graphic-block and window-map uploads through it. The group is
solo with entry seam: its callers are still the guest's, and --no-replace
is the control that restores the guest's body.

The shape is measured. A whole-routine dispatch freezes the clock until it
returns, so (1) the VDP's data-port FIFO slots and dma_busy_until would be
the guest's only by accident -- the corpus compares both, and
UploadSpriteTable, ProcessDmaQueue, WriteVramWords and ClearCram write the
data port or start transfers; (2) mdloop_run() services a VBlank at an
instruction boundary, so a dispatch that covers a routine pushes the
interrupt, and the frame number the sound is advanced at, to its end (the
first version moved one YM2612 write's frame stamp at frame 1547); and
(3) QueueDmaTransfer's full queue waits a VBlank and FlushDmaQueue's body
is a spin only the drain can end. So every row answers one guest
instruction per dispatch, charged at its own address.

The mechanism needed one change for a group this hot. The seam charged a
replacement one clock cycle at a time -- finer than the guest, which does
it once per instruction -- and with 4.8 M of a 2 700-frame route's 31 M
instructions going through the seam that moved the Z80's register writes
across the sound's sample boundaries: 587 623 differing samples against the
engine equivalence check's bound of 400. A replacement may now record what
each guest instruction cost (md68k_replacement_charge) and the seam charges
those, at the guest's own boundaries (D95). A replacement that records
nothing is unchanged, so the leaf helpers' and the screens' measurements
stand.

Measured. tests/test_vdp_library.sh, house-selection 2 700 frames and the
Atreides mission 9 500: instructions, cycles, picture, work RAM, audio and
the YM2612 timeline all identical, the instruction count unchanged (a span
removes no step), --no-replace byte for byte against legacy, no fault, and
the mission's checkpoints inside the route. Twenty-five of the rows carry
the committed per-instruction fixture a recording reaches
(analysis/fixtures/vdp-library/manifest.txt has the hashes, and each
replays against the C, the live guest and the fuzz); on the house-selection
route the group answers 4 831 763 guest instructions over 22 of its 29
rows.

Two findings the fixtures measured. ClearBackdrop $000BDC is not a routine:
the eight-byte register write there has no rts before FadePaletteToBlack
$000BE4, so the two are entry points into one body -- a jsr here clears the
backdrop and then runs the whole fade -- which is why it is not a row and
why the fades are the frame clock's slice. And the splitter has a hole at
$000F0E-$000F52, the drain's last block, which the disassembly calls data
and the interpreter decodes; the row answers it with the listing's
instructions and says so beside them.

The four rows no committed route enters (SetWindowVramBase, SetWindowBase,
LoadWindowTiles, LoadWindowTilesB -- the mission's lost/won/end screens and
the briefing pages) are proved from constructed states in
port/tests/test_vdp_library.c, ticket 130's precedent, and declared with
that reason in tests/test_vdp_library.sh and tests/test_oracle.sh.

D95 records the step record and why the group is a transcription rather
than a design; docs/next-engine.md, "The VDP library", has the
measurements, and docs/reverse-engineering-notes.md the routine boundaries.
docs/html is rebuilt from the tree, the flag inventory regenerated, and
the group added to docs/port-zones.json, analysis/porting-stages.json and
D91's table.
