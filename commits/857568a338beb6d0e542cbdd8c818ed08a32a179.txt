181: the data formats, decoded and re-encoded
The ticket's premise was that extraction needs a format and the formats were
only half known. This pass establishes five of them as code: a decoder and an
encoder for each, and the round trip that makes them formats rather than
descriptions -- decode the real region out of rom/original, re-encode it, and
the bytes must come back.

scripts/rom_formats.py is the one codec, and the check:

  compression   DecompressRle $000F8C's whole language: a short back-reference
                relative to the write pointer, literals, a $FE byte-run fill,
                absolute back-references ($C0-$FD and the 16-bit $FF), and the
                $80 end marker. The cartridge loads two streams ($001800 and
                $001C16); both re-encode byte for byte with an encoder whose
                choices are recovered from them (longest match, nearest on a
                tie, fills only over 66, literals to 63).
  resources     the 15 sixteen-byte entries at $02243A field by field, the
                destination-selector byte, the two palette markers and the
                window map; every entry and every block list it names.
  graphics      the nibble-packed tiles, the C-RAM palette, the plane/window
                map cell word, the 80-entry attribute table and the 12-byte
                sprite cells, plus the facing table $08424A (49 entries). The
                pictures are drawn: resource 0 is the house selection screen.
  missions      the five missions x five sides at $021866, the 12 opcodes of
                $00C172's dispatcher with their operand sizes, the terminator,
                and the WSA name chunks the scripts branch over. All 25 scripts
                round-trip; 20 of them end exactly where the next side's script
                begins, which is what pins the operand sizes.
  music         the two pointer blocks, the voice/song/effect/drum tables (all
                little-endian: the Z80 reads them byte for byte), the 39-byte
                voice record and the register program at $15F5, the sequence
                event encoding (notes, $60-$72 opcodes, $80/$C0-tagged lengths)
                and the note ladder at $1141/$115B. With a recorded timeline the
                port wrote (--ym-log), each voice burst is named back to a record
                (8 of 8) and each frequency word to the ladder (17 of 17).

Two conventions the data uses are now written down: the bank byte in every
32-bit pointer (masked with $1FFFFF, and read for meaning by the resource
table), and a block list's size being tiles in an UploadGraphicBlocks list but
bytes in a resource list.

Six pages, docs/formats.md as the index and one per format. Each ends with the
command that runs its round trip and a "what is not established" section: the
compressor's choice policy is recovered from two streams; the music's schedule
and the volume-scaled total-level ladder are the driver's and are documented
rather than modelled; three opcode fields are named by the byte they write.

The checks: a fast-tier check (18 s, no build, no emulator) with six patched
dumps and a synthetic timeline as the controls that the round trips can fail,
and a slow-tier check that produces the -O1 harness's register log from the
committed house-selection recording and reproduces it from the data.

No source, asset or dump change: rom/original is untouched and the rebuild is
the same as before this commit.

Decisions made where the ticket was silent:
* The compressor got an encoder, not only a decoder: the ticket asks for a
  re-encode, and a decoder alone cannot make it byte-exact. Its policy is
  stated as recovered (not as the original tool's implementation).
* The fast tier runs the data round trips and the slow tier the register
  stream, because the latter needs the harness's --ym-log; the ticket allows
  "a named slower check".
* The mission opcodes' meanings are read from the handlers and stated with
  their addresses; where the code names a field but not its meaning, the page
  says so instead of guessing.
