ticket 200: the machine has one execution path and the corpus leaves the build
`port/generated/` is deleted: 88 files, 162 275 lines, 9.5 MB of one `case` per
68000 instruction, committed since ticket 74 and linked into ten binaries. It
was not faster than the interpreter once the interpreter was compiled at the
same -O2 (33 s against 37 s on the 9 500-frame mission route) and it could only
ever prove the guest ran; the port's C is the game now, and the evidence it
produced is banked under `analysis/` (D130).

The machine is left with one path. `md68k_translated_step()`,
`md68k_set_translation()`, `md68k_translation_enabled()`, `MD68K_FALLBACK` and
the `translated` counter are gone; `md68k_step()` charges the instruction's cost
and hands it to `md68k_unimplemented()`, which is the decoder and executor that
used to be the fallback. `interpreted + replaced = steps` is the whole
instruction mix, which is a statement the gates can check. The function keeps
its ticket-45 name because the port's C and its transcribed parts call it where
they hand a `dc.w` word back; its header says so.

The suite stops building generated code: `md68k-sweep` (39 008 addresses through
both paths), the build-time transpile of `tests/fixtures/fixtures.asm`, and
`test_translated.c`'s second run all go. `test_translated.c` becomes
`test_instructions.c`: the same real-ROM instructions and PRM expectations, run
through the machine's one path. The fixture counts fall (728 -> 324, 143 555 ->
142 412) because the duplicated runs are gone, not because a statement was
dropped.

`port/generated/` is git-ignored, `make -C port translation` still writes it
from the committed disassembly in two seconds, and
`scripts/transcribe_group.py --check` regenerates it when a check needs it.

The moveq fix the removal exposed, the re-recorded fixtures and the gates' new
arithmetic are the next two commits.
