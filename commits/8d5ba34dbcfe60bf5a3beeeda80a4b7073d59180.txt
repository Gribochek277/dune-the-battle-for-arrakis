188: the order machine is a group, and its transcription is generated
The order machine, in C: the script interpreter `RunOrderScript` $00CBFC with
its nineteen actions, the programs' function library `RunOrderScriptHelper`
$000828, the 53 game actions the 64-entry table `$022750` names, the fifteen
early-bank actions, the movement step `MoveUnit` $01BBD0 and the AI's group
layer `RunAiSides` $018DF2 with its twelve routines. Six rows, one group,
`orders`, `solo` with entry `seam` (D109), zero delegations: 4 384 addresses
answered by the C, and a `dc.w` word inside a span answered by the machine's
interpreter, which is what the guest's own pc would do (D41).

Four thousand cases are not written by hand. `scripts/transpile_next.py` emits
`port/next/order_*.c` from the verified disassembly and *imports* the
translation's emitters, so a form the translation handles is a form the orders
handle and the two cannot drift; `--check` is the gate that keeps the committed
files the disassembly's. The generator was validated by regenerating ticket
187's hand-written `next/units_order.c` and diffing it case for case: every
charge, every call out and every boundary came out identical. What is not
generated is the readable layer -- the contract, the order ids, the AI and the
movement rules (`order_machine.h`), the entry's layout and the script's
vocabulary (`order_machine_shared.h`), the chain and the port's own number
(`order_machine.c`) -- and a change to a decision is a change to those files.

The pair is byte-exact. The battle route (26 000 frames of the Atreides mission
with no input), legacy against `next --replace-group orders`: picture, work RAM
(sha256 e673bb0e), the VDP's write counters and DMA counts, the Z80's state, the
YM2612 timeline, the clock (3 330 716 164 cycles) and the interpreted count
(4 910 796) identical, and the translation smaller by exactly the 6 876 181
instructions the C stood in for -- 450 472 dispatches (RunOrderScript 276 600,
RunOrderScriptHelper 58 070, OrderActionTrap 40 177, OrderActionSetDelay 23 595,
MoveUnit 29 385, RunAiSides 22 645). `--no-replace` is legacy byte for byte.

The ticket's own instrument is new: `--order-log` writes one line per unit
record whose order fields changed at a frame boundary, and
`scripts/compare_order_traces.py` compares two runs as "the same order, on the
same unit, at the same frame, with the same target". The two traces are
identical line for line (4 938 lines) -- the end state cannot say that, because
a unit that reached the same cell by another route has the same picture.
`--order-patience 240` is the port's own number and the demonstration: it scales
the delay every program asks through `OrderActionSetDelay`, and the comparator
shows the same order on the same unit at a different frame. The cartridge has no
aggression number to alter -- `AiScanForTarget` takes the nearest unit of its
class and never compares a distance with a threshold -- and that finding is on
the rules page rather than invented in the code.

The AI's group layer is the one part no committed mission reaches (nothing
creates a group; all seven records at `$FF789C` stay zero for the whole route),
so it is declared unmeasured with that reason and its states are the constructed
ones. The corpus's four per-instruction fixtures for this region are deleted
with the shape they measured, as ticket 187 deleted the units'.

Fixtures: `analysis/fixtures/orders/manifest.txt` banks four states of the
route (the mission's first orders, the enemy's march, a harvester's harvest, the
survivors) with the units' orders beside them, and
`port/tests/test_order_machine.c` constructs the two situations the route cannot
show (a three-word script through its own tables -- the decode, the dispatch,
the action and the delay it writes -- and the trap stopping at a frame
boundary). `tests/test_orders.sh` is the gate; the rules page gains §6b (the
order machine's six rules, the ids and the movement rules M1-M6); D109 records
the three decisions (generated transcription, the trace as the instrument, the
port's own pacing number) and R4's bound now skips a generated file, which is
what its own words said it was for.

Gates: `make -C port test`, `scripts/build.sh`, the fast tier, this check.
