174: the feature flag is per subsystem, and the truth is countable
One flag per routine is the unit the screens needed and the wrong one for the
game. `NEXT_REPLACEMENT_MAX` capped a selection at 32 entries, `--replace NAME`
could not say "the record subsystem, all of it", and nothing distinguished the
guest's code from the port's C from C that hands work back to the guest one
instruction at a time -- a subsystem that reads as ported while a third of its
addresses still runs the transliterated body.

A row of the replacement table now names its **flag group**, a subsystem named
with D91's stage-record ids (`analysis/porting-stages.json`, ticket 175):
`next_replacement_enable_group()` / `--replace-group NAME` enables every row that
names it and only those, so a group cannot be half on and a screen plus the
helpers only it calls comes as one selector. `tests/test_stages.py` now also
reads the row's group field and the table's own group list, so the record, the
flag and the page cannot disagree: every row's group is the group the record
lists it in, and a group's `complete` flag is the record's `delegations: 0`. The
mechanism enforces that claim -- a complete group's implementation that returns
`next_replacement_delegate()` is `MD68K_FAULT_REPLACEMENT` naming the group and
the address -- which is ticket 174's acceptance rule in the one form that
survives D91's stricter staging (solo also needs fixtures and the playtest route,
so every group today is `paired`; `input` and `guest-text` are complete).

The selection is one slot per row and "every implementation" is a mode
(`next_replacement_enable_all()`, the front ends' `replacements_all`), so the cap
and the fixed-array expansion of the default are gone: a run of `--engine next`
installs all 30 rows, and the option lists are selector lists, not a policy
(raised to 64, with the state file's format bound alongside).

The truth is measured, not declared. With `--flag-inventory` the dispatch
records every address it sees inside a row's region -- the guest's cost of the
instruction from the machine's own cycle model, whether the C answered it or
handed it back, and the addresses themselves -- and writes it as the `flags`
block of the harness's JSON. `scripts/porting_inventory.py` runs the committed
route (three recordings to the catalog's last checkpoint on each), joins the
record with the committed listing (815 routines) and writes
`analysis/flag-inventory.json`: every routine with its address, group and state
(735 guest, 61 the port's C, 19 C with delegations at this commit), and per
group the addresses, the delegations still owed and the instruction and cycle
cost of both sides -- e.g. the pad protocol's 649 728 C steps standing in for
30 537 216 guest instructions at 171 027 828 cycles a side, and the title
screen's 436 addresses still handed back.

The **porting dashboard** page (ticket 175's) now renders that document too,
beside D91's stage table: per group the addresses, the delegations owed and the
cost of both sides, the rows, and all 815 routines with their group and state.
The page still needs no port binary, no route and no clock, so it stays a
committed byte-identical rebuild. The recipe for one screen (ticket 175's staged
version) ends on the dashboard.

`tests/test_flag_groups.sh` (slow tier, ~11 minutes) is the gate the inventory
turns into a rule. It requires the committed inventory to be the tree's, then
fails when a group the record calls complete still delegates, when an address
belongs to two groups, when a routine has no single group and state, when a row
is not enabled by the default selection, or when the flag cannot select exactly
one group. It finishes by booting the catalog's checkpoints with all eight groups
on and with none: with ticket 115's cycle charges off the only picture that
differs is the title screen's (the port's own row -- a feature, asserted to
differ), and with them on the briefing text's reveal is one character ahead, the
seam's per-cycle charging that the audio comparison already bounds. Any other
checkpoint differing fails, so a group cannot be load-bearing by accident.

The mechanism's own fixtures (`make -C port test`) add the group table and the
uncapped selection, `next_replacement_check()` (no address claimed twice), the
audit's slots and the JSON report, and the complete-group fault with its control.
Three existing checks patched an exact row's text and were updated to find the
row by what it says (and to name no group, as a fixture's row). The four slow
checks that exercise the mechanism (engine equivalence, the state file, guest
text, the options screen, the mission cursor) are green, as are the fast tier,
`tests/test_stages.py`, `tests/test_docs.sh` and `tests/test_pipeline_inputs.py`.
