ticket 214: the engine plays the driver's own stream, and the routes are its gate
The owner heard the music as "some sounds, but not what the original was".
The comparator agreed: on the front end's recording the engine's audio was a
different arrangement -- FAIL (time shift), B delayed by 780 ms, window 17's
dominant peak 330 Hz against the driver's 524 Hz, the envelopes correlating at
+0.056, 53 of 198 windows inside the tolerance -- while
tests/test_audio_engine.sh stayed green, because its per-piece report was
printed and not gated and its only route pair was a short one that passed.

The cause, measured: the engine's register stream agreed with the driver's for
116 writes and diverged at the 117th, where it wrote the same block one
destination lower ($B0 against $B1). Reading the Z80 listing (the Ghidra
listing of $08514A) against next/audio_engine.c found five differences, each
one fixed against the driver's own code and measured on the harness pair
(port/build/audio-engine --driver against --engine, mission song 0):

* a length does not stop the stream read ($04BC): the note or opcode that
  follows is handled in the same tick -- the one-tick lag every note had;
* the FM pool is the driver's order 0,1,4,5,6,2 with the destination in the
  entry's low three bits ($176C), not the pool index, which made the third note
  take dest 2 and the fourth dest 3 (a channel that does not exist);
* a destination that was in use is keyed off before it is reprogrammed
  ($1425), so the envelope retriggers;
* opcode 6C's two bytes are one 16-bit value: the low byte is the fractional
  step and the high byte the whole-semitone offset ($1081), where the first
  byte was being added as semitones -- 15 semitones sharp in the transposed
  sections;
* the frequency flush writes the destinations whose channel is dirty, set by
  6C and the effects and cleared by the flush ($0F4F/$0F8F/$1073), not a
  last-destination bookkeeping that never cleared.

It also found what ticket 197 had left out: the per-frame PSG envelope mixer
($0066, four channels of attack/decay/sustain/release on an 8-bit
attenuation), the odd ladder ($115B is 64 words, not the 12 the asset file
carries), the effect pass's own deltas ($0EA1) and the kit player ($130D): a
drum record is twelve bytes, its bytes 1-5 a 24-bit offset from the drum table
and its bytes 6-7 the sample's length, streamed to the YM2612's DAC every
measured 726 68000 cycles. A sample written at the engine's frame boundary
would be sample-and-held for a whole frame, so chips/sound.c grew
md68k_sound_set_dac_stream(): a source hands the module one byte per period and
the module applies it at the cycle it is due, inside its own interval loop.
The comparison build never installs one, so legacy's audio is untouched.

Measured after: on the harness pair all 5 950 YM2612 writes are identical in
order and value and its audio passes at every threshold (envelope r +0.922, 40
of 40 windows). The routes a player hears, at 6 dB / 100 cents / envelope 0.6 /
local shift 20 ms: house-selection 167 of 201 windows within tolerance, every
level window inside 6 dB, r +0.816; the mission 127 of 174, r +0.822. The
per-piece report is 67 of 113 inside the tolerance, none silent (38 before).

The gate: tests/test_audio_engine.sh now compares both routes. The level and
the envelope are hard; the pitch is held to the floor the frame-grained model
reaches (70% and 60%) because the engine's registers land at the VBlank where
the driver's land at their own cycle -- a measured mean of 31 807 cycles in --
and a tick's note can round to the frame before or after; with LFO-modulated
voices the comparator's dominant peak then picks a different partial. The
commit says so rather than weakening the level. The report keeps a floor too,
and names the pieces outside it (most of them kit-using songs, where the
harness's driver mode cannot pace the DAC: no 68000 ticks the Z80's frame flag,
so the driver writes one byte a frame).

Also: D110 in docs/decisions.md, the audio sections of docs/next-engine.md and
docs/native-port.md, the dashboard's frame cost regenerated (2.62 ms on the
port's path against 6.04 ms for the guest, ratio 2.31), the site rebuilt.
