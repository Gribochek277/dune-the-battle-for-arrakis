Z80: bit y,(hl) must not write back, and the register stream says so
The vendored core's exec_opcode_cb() wrote the byte `bit y,(hl)` had just
read back to the same address, where the Z80 only reads. Upstream's zexdoc
and zexall cannot see it -- writing the same byte back leaves RAM
unchanged -- but the cartridge's sound driver polls the YM2612's status at
$4000 with `bit 7,(hl)` (BUSY) and `bit 0,(hl)` (timer A), so the write-back
stored the status byte in the chip's address latch and the next data byte
went to register $80 or $01 instead of the register the driver had latched.
The driver's timer-paced DAC playback then ran without its timer reset and
drifted away from the reference: 20 s into a 132 s mission run the port's
register stream carried a $001 = $15 for every $027 = $15 the emulator's
own VGM log had (129 621 extra writes), found by the comparison ticket 68
added.

The fix is one condition in that write-back. The DD/FD CB operand form
(`bit y,(ix+d)`, the driver's BUSY wait) already guarded the BIT case, and
the rest of the core's write-backs are genuine writes. z80.h is untouched;
z80.c's upstream and local sha256, the reason and the regression fixture
(tests/test_z80.c, "bit (hl) does not write back") are recorded in
port/runtime/z80/README.md, and tools/REVISIONS.txt now says the vendored
files carry one local patch rather than being verbatim.

With the core patched the same mission run's stream has 902 412 writes
against the reference's 897 252 and is the same sequence for the first
12 375 writes (frames 250-1 220); the remainder is the driver's DAC burst
timing under the port's Z80 timing model (mdz80.h), not the chip -- driving
both cores with the reference's own write stream for the first 12 s gives
636 912 samples with maximum difference 0. docs/native-port.md, "Audio,
slice 2", records the long-run measurement and its limit.

make -C port test: 36 177 checks, 0 failures (the new fixture included);
tests/test_audio_compare.sh, tests/test_ym_log.sh and
tests/test_recording.sh all pass.
