ticket 152: the wide camera's bound is the camera's, and the scroll is measured
The mission map's scroll showed blocks of terrain that belong nowhere along the
edge it pushed into view. The cause is one word written in the wrong direction:
ticket 132's wide view moves the guest's bounds by the extra columns, and it
wrote `24 - extra` into the view record's own x word (`$FFFFBF12 + $8`,
`$FFFFBF1A`). That word is the *cursor's* 24-pixel margin in
`UpdatePlacementCursor` (`$003BD6`, where the record's x is the cursor's screen
position), but it is the *camera's* bound in `UpdateMissionCursor` `$0030FA`:
`$0033BC`/`$0033C2` clamp the record's x with `+$8`, and `$0033F4` adds the map
origin `$FFFFE3EC` to it and turns it into the cursor's cell `$FFFFC240`. With
`24 - extra` there, the mission handler's camera ran `extra` (107 at 16:9)
pixels past the map's left edge, where the plane's cells are its 8 KiB ring's
far side -- the map's right-hand terrain, drawn at the picture's left.

The fix is to put that word back on the camera's side of the line: it, and the
map view's own limit `$FFFFE3D2` (`$003BC8`), become their guest bases plus the
extra, so the camera stops with the picture's left column on the map's left
column. `24 - extra` stays where it belongs, the placement handler's *cursor*
clamp, which its C reads from next_mission_wide_cursor_left() and which is
unchanged. At 0 extra all three are the guest's own values, so the 4:3 run is
byte-identical (measured: the legacy and next 4:3 runs' VDP dumps identical).

What proves it, and what the plane holds outside the drawn window:

* tests/test_mission_map_scroll.sh (new, slow tier) -- the four directions
  (right, left, up, down) held from entry 7 700 to 7 960, compared with
  `legacy` (the guest's own code) frame by frame: the picture, the 64 KiB of
  work RAM, the raw VDP state and the whole 7 960-entry `--vdp-log` identical,
  with 248/114/44/44 entries moving the hscroll; both edges at 7 990 (the
  camera at the map's limits 512 and 1215, the view record at 24 and 296, the
  picture the guest's); and the wide picture's band measured against the
  guest's *frame* for the same world columns (entry 7 790 wide against entry
  7 745, the same world 107 px left: 0 of 102 720 pixels differ; the control's
  emptied plane column differs in 4 544, so the comparison can see a stale
  band).
* tests/test_mission_wide.sh -- a new last case: a held left scroll in the
  16:9 window stops the view record at 131 (24 + 107) with the camera at 647,
  inside the map's 619. The rest of the ticket's cases still pass (the 4:3,
  16:9 and 21:9 pictures, the guest's clock and RAM, the band's appended unit).
* port/tests/test_mission_wide.c -- the fixture pins the two camera words at
  base + 107 and the cursor's accessor at 24 - 107, so the two can no longer be
  confused silently.

docs/reverse-engineering-notes.md, "The map plane outside the drawn window, and
the camera that walks it (ticket 152)" records what the plane holds there (the
columns the camera has passed, the guest's own, 0 of 102 720 pixels different),
the two handlers' use of the record's x, the correction and the measurements;
