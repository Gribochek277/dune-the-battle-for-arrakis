The menu's arrow slides, and the slide is a module
The game's OPTIONS screen does not jump its cursor between rows: it slides it
four pixels a frame across a sixteen-pixel pitch, and it says so with sound 25
when the row the screen acts on changes and 40 when a press chooses one. The
port's own settings screen computed its sprite's Y straight from the selected
row and moved instantly and silently, because nothing had measured the game.

Ticket 137 makes the measurement a module: port/next/menu_cursor.c owns a list
(its length and its first row's Y), the row the screen acts on, the pixel
position the sprite is drawn at, the pixels still to travel, a settled flag, the
line of the window the cursor's row sits on, and the sounds one frame owes. It
has no VDP and no guest CPU in it -- a screen hands it the pad's meaning and
takes back a Y and a sound mask -- so the OPTIONS rewrite (138) adopts it and
PORT SETTINGS and GAMEPAD MAPPING (139, 140) follow without touching it.

What the measuring found, and what is written next to the code:

* $FFFFBECC is the sprite's Y, not a private copy: $0013EA reads the record at
  that address as the sprite's position.
* $FFFFBED2 is the signed distance still to travel; $0115BE takes four off it and
  gives four to the Y each frame, so a move is four frames and the press frame
  is the first of them.
* $FFFFBED6 is the row, and the list's *length* is the screen's datum: the same
  routine walks seven rows from the front end's OPTIONS row and nine from the
  mission (which draws RESTART MISSION and GAME TIME), wrapping at $84 and $A4
  respectively. The module takes the length from the list.
* The ends do not slide: a press past an end puts the cursor on the other end's
  position in place, on the press frame.
* A press during a slide is dropped -- the opposite direction and the choice
  button alike ($0114F8) -- and the slide finishes where it was going.
* The move sound is one frame behind the cursor: the settle frame's selector
  still sees a non-zero counter, so 25 arrives on the frame after the last step.
* Not every row plays 40: NEW CONTROL's handler toggles its scheme silently. The
  module reports the *choice*; the 40 belongs to the row.

The acceptance is the trajectory, so the trajectory is the fixture.
scripts/capture_cursor_trace.py runs the guest's own code on the port's machine
(--engine legacy, whose byte-for-byte agreement with next is the engine
equivalence check) and reads the OPTIONS screen's four words and both sound
pushes out of the work-RAM access log, one line per frame of the screen's loop.
Two routes, because the screen has two lists: 286 frames from the front end and
496 from the mission, committed under analysis/cursor-traces/. The capture
refuses a trace that is not one loop iteration per line or whose position,
counter and row disagree, and re-capturing either file reproduces it byte for
byte.

port/tests/test_menu_cursor.c replays both through the module frame for frame --
position, counter, row and sounds -- and covers what the traces cannot carry: the
clamp of a list that does not wrap, and the window line at every scroll position
of a thirteen-row list (ticket 117's difference, never the sum).
tests/test_menu_cursor.sh re-captures both fixtures from the cartridge, runs the
replay and holds the control: a copy of the module that steps eight pixels a
frame must fail the trajectory. The module's contract, the measurements and the
two routes are in docs/next-engine.md ("The menu cursor"); the site is rebuilt.
