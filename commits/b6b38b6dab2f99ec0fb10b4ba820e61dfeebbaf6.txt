Z80: the sound CPU runs at its own clock (ticket 70)
The port ran one Z80 instruction after every 68000 instruction, which is not a
clock: the sound CPU's speed became a function of the guest's instruction mix.
md68k_step() now calls mdz80_run() after every 68000 instruction, which runs
whole Z80 instructions until the Z80's clock passes the machine's -- one
T-state is 15 master clocks against the 68000's 7 (MD68K_MCLKS_PER_Z80,
BlastEm's own MCLKS_PER_Z80/MCLKS_PER_68K), so a nop is 8.57 68000 cycles and
the driver's average instruction is 20.5. While the Z80 is in reset or the
68000 owns its bus its clock is levelled, not banked: a long bus hold is not a
debt it pays back in a burst when it is let go. mdz80_step() is still one
instruction, which is what the driver fixtures drive.

The per-instruction cost is measured, not assumed. port/tests/z80_timings.txt
is scripts/z80_timing_probe.py's comparison of the port's core against BlastEm
v1.0.0's own (its z80.cpu through its cpu_dsl.py in interpreter mode): every
complete encoding of the base map and of CB, ED, DD, FD, DD CB and FD CB, each
with every flag clear and set, 3 200 cases, one instruction each from the same
registers and memory. 3 134 agree exactly; 66 differ (33 encodings, worst 11
T-states) and the table commits both columns, so the error is in the file. The
port keeps the pinned core's numbers because they are the Z80's documented
timings (INC SP 6, CB (HL) rotate 15, INC IX 10, RETN 14, CPI 16) where
BlastEm's core simplifies (4, 14, 8, 8, 18); port/tests/test_z80.c runs all
3 200 cases through the core on every `make -C port test` and requires the port
column to be what it charges, so the model cannot drift silently.

Measured on the 7 898-frame mission replay: the Z80 executes 49 342 322
instructions against 93 917 720 (its clock now covers 99.7 % of the machine's
time, against 196.1 % -- the old model ran it at twice the hardware's speed),
while the 68000's own trajectory does not move (94 160 928 instructions and
1 013 551 514 cycles against 94 161 164 and 1 013 551 508). The driver's
timer-paced DAC bursts now match the reference's within +-10 writes of 12 000-
14 000 (0.07 %) where the old model wrote 3.3-5 % too many, and its 3 000-frame
host cost falls from 8.40 s to 8.00 s.

The music's note writes ($28, $A0/A4/B0) are the reference's in the same order
over the whole 71 s the two machines share a game state, on plateaus of +2.77
frames (spread 1.0) and -2.26 frames, with the step at the screen change -- and
the old model's offsets are the same to 0.02 frames, so the ~40 ms is the
port's cycle estimate of the boot (the port's first VBlank entry is at machine
frame 11), not the sound CPU. The 300-frame house-selection gate pair still
passes ticket 68's thresholds, with a better envelope correlation (+0.855
against +0.805); the mission pair passes 180 of 528 windows at the same
thresholds (166 before), 257 with ticket 70's --local-shift 0.02 and 351 with
60 ms, and its remaining windows are the reference's analog-ish output chain
(the port's share of the energy above 3 390 Hz is up to 8x the
reference's, its level up to +5.4 dB on bright notes), the DAC bursts' residue and the post-press divergence.

The one thing the clock moved: the checkpoint catalog's animated-element phase.
The briefing portrait is now in step at 5 398 (0 pixels against 67) and a unit
icon is one step apart at 7 898 and 8 148 (182 and 152 pixels against 75 and 45)
-- the game's handshake waits now cost what the hardware charges and the port's
estimated 68000 cycle model no longer has the sound CPU's error to cancel
against. analysis/checkpoints.json records the new measurements and the reason,
with the thresholds moved by the measured amount.

docs/native-port.md's Z80 timing paragraph is replaced by the model, the
measurement and what is still approximation (bus contention, the interrupt at
an instruction boundary, the last instruction's overshoot).
