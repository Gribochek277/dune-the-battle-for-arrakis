port: fix the interpreter's MOVEP direction
The decoder used only bits 7-6 of the opmode as its "shape", which drops the
direction bit, and then mapped shape < 2 to the register-to-memory direction:
the two directions came out inverted. MOVEP's opmode is bits 8-6, where 4/5 are
memory-to-register (word/long) and 6/7 register-to-memory.

The differential harness found it: the VDP command queue at $FFFFC5A8 is patched
by `movep.l d5,(-7,a0)` at $000E36, and with the direction inverted the port
loaded zeros into D5 instead of writing the two bytes, so frame 3 kept the queue
bytes the boot wrote ($94009700/$96009500) while the reference had
$9400977F/$96809500. BlastEm's answer for that instruction was taken from its GDB
stub, single-stepped at $000E36: the low bytes of the queued commands become $7F
and $80. With the fix the port's frames 1, 2 and 3 are byte-identical to the
reference.

port/tests/test_isa.c gains `isa: movep direction and size`: all four forms
(word/long, both directions) with hand-computed expectations (the high-order
byte first, a word operation only changing the lower 16 bits of the register,
the bytes in between left alone).
