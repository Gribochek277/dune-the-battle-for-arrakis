171: the pointer's scroll is a screen speed, not the guest's one-pixel loop
The playtest's slow-and-jerky camera came from the pointer's frame driving the
cartridge's edge-scroll loops: their `distance / 16` moved the view one guest
pixel a frame (60 a second at 320 wide) and their remainder
`$FFFFBF3C`/`$FFFFBF3E` alternated the visible step between one pixel and none.

The band's subtraction now hands the guest's loop zero and the port's own pace
moves the view with ticket 170's `next_mission_map_view_scroll_by()`:
`next/mission_pointer_scroll.c` owns the constant -- 8 **screen** pixels a guest
frame, 480 a second at 60 Hz, one and a half 320-wide pictures -- divided by the
scale the map is drawn at (8 guest pixels at 1x, 4 at 2x, 3 at 3x, 2 at 4x,
rounded to the nearest and never below 1). The scale is
`play_display_picture_scale()`: the world region's in the wide mission, the
picture's in fit-height, `--scale` on the reference path. The x band test makes
the frame's one call with both axes (a corner is the guest's own two tests, not
a normalised diagonal); the y test calls only when x asked for nothing. The
camera moves once a frame, the first frame in the band moves the same as the
tenth, the frame the hand leaves moves nothing, and the cursor's record stays
the hand's pixel -- the d-pad's pull against the view is not the mouse's.
