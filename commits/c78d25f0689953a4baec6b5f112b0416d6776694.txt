port: VDP, DMA completion and the rendered frame (ticket 43)
Implement the Mega Drive VDP in the port (port/runtime/vdp.c): the register
file, VRAM/CRAM/VSRAM, the data and control ports with the two-word command
latch, the status register with the DMA-busy bit the game polls at $000E68,
all three DMA kinds (68k memory, fill, copy) and a 320x240 renderer (planes
A/B with scroll, the window plane, sprites, the priority comparison). The
picture is written by port/runtime/png.c, a dependency-free PNG writer.

The harness can render the frame it reached (--png) and dump the raw VDP
state (--vdp-dump); port/build/vdp-render renders a dump without a guest, and
make -C port now builds the harness (it is the binary that writes the PNG).

The frame clock spends a frame's worth of guest instructions (FRAME_BUDGET,
--frame-budget) before a wait becomes a VBlank. Without it the guest's
two-instruction wait for the frame counter ticked the counter hundreds of
times per emulated frame, so the port ran the whole intro orders of magnitude
too fast.

Evidence:
- scripts/capture_frame.py captures BlastEm's picture at a chosen VBlank
  entry: a windowed emulator on a private Xvfb display (X11 forced, Wayland
  dropped, private HOME, pty for stdin/stderr so warnings cannot become a
  desktop dialog), driven by the GDB stub.
- scripts/compare_frame.py compares that with the port's PNG. Frame 10 (the
  SEGA logo) is pixel-identical: 0 of 76800 pixels differ. tests/test_frame.sh
  runs that comparison and a black-frame negative case, and is part of
  tests/run_all.sh.
- The port's VDP state at frames 10, 400 and 600 equals BlastEm's event-log
  state: 0 differing CRAM entries, 1 VRAM byte.
- compare_port_state.py --frames 10 reaches frame 10. Frames 4, 6, 7, 11, 12
  and 100 match byte for byte; 5, 8, 9 and 10 differ only in the interrupted
  PC byte on the stack ($0012BC vs $0012B8, both inside the frame-counter
  wait), which is the rendezvous, not the state.

The boot fixture's register expectation moves with the device: the boot writes
$8104 and $8F02 after the table and starts a VRAM fill, so reg1/reg15 and the
fill's length counter differ from the table.

docs/native-port.md documents the device, the evidence, what is deliberately
not implemented (no cycle model, interlace, shadow/highlight, H-interrupt,
sprite overflow/collision, 128K VRAM, byte-lane control queue, Mode 4) and the
next blocker: the frame clock is an instruction budget, so the intro diverges
by frame 800-1000 and later screens cannot be compared frame for frame yet.
