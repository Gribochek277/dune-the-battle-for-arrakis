173: the oracle is the transliterated code, one dispatch at a time
The port's proof that a routine's C reproduces the guest's was a whole recording
run twice: the same route as the guest and as the C, and the work RAM, picture,
chip writes and clock compared. That is a strong claim about one route and a weak
one about the routine -- it says nothing about the states the route never
entered, and when it fails it says "somewhere in 2 700 frames these two runs
differ". What did not exist was the guest's half of a unit test: a way to ask
"what states was this routine ever entered in, and what came out of each".

`port/app/oracle_corpus.{c,h}` is that, in the shape the differential sweep
already proved for single instructions -- a deterministic state, one dispatch, a
full comparison. The unit is **one dispatch of the replacement seam**: the state
the hook was handed at one address and the state it left behind, which is exactly
the contract and makes both shapes of row one rule. A row with `span == 0`
answers its whole routine in one call, so its fixture is entered at the entry
address and ends at the return address the caller pushed; a row with a span
answers one guest instruction per call, so its fixture holds one entry per
execution of an address in the span. Nested registered routines are inside a
leaf's dispatch, because the machine's hook never sees the addresses in between.

The machine gained the two instruments the measurement needs. The work-RAM hook
already existed; `md68k_set_bus_hook()` is its other half (every non-RAM access,
once per access, with the region) and `md68k_set_step_hook()` sees every
instruction before and after it runs. The "after" call is *inside* the
instruction -- before the frame clock can deliver a VBlank and before the driver
runs the Z80 for the instruction's cycles -- because the record must be the
instruction's: a leaf the frame clock interrupts mid-routine is counted
(`interrupted`) and left out instead of recorded wrong, and between two
instructions of a leaf the recorder stops sampling so the Z80's own accesses are
not attributed to it. Both install nothing by default and `legacy` installs
nothing.

A fixture record holds the entry state (the 68000's registers, SR and USP; the
frame point; the pad's wire state and the VDP's bus-visible state when the
dispatch read them; the first-touch values of every work-RAM byte it read or
wrote) and the outcome (the exit state, the same bytes' after-values, the chip
accesses in order, and the guest's own instruction cost -- the number the
replacement contract makes the C report). Entries are folded by their entry
state, and the two fields the fold leaves out are the ones no guest read can see:
how long ago the pad's last TH edge was past the six-button timeout, and the VDP's
frame counter -- a C that branches on `call.frame` where the guest branched on
nothing is a C that has invented state.

`port/build/oracle-capture` runs a recording (or `--press` route) on the guest and
writes one committed fixture per registered row; `port/build/oracle-run` replays
one against its C and makes three comparisons, because the two oracles can fail
apart: the C against the recording (the corpus), the live guest against the
recording (a stale fixture is named as one), and the C against the live guest
(the whole-recording claim, per routine). The same set then drives the C under
deterministically mutated states -- flags, a register, an address register in
RAM, RAM bytes, the pad's buttons and type -- compared between the two live
implementations, faults included. The arms the fuzz reaches and no recorded entry
did are printed as uncovered, and so is a conditional branch with only one arm
ever seen; the pad's type is mutated so the controller read's six-button branch
takes the other side, which the recordings never do.

The comparison runs in a fixed order (fault, exit pc, registers, RAM window,
window completeness, chip accesses, cost) and names the first divergence with the
entry's pc, frame and cycle point -- `recorded vs C: cost: 360 cycles vs 361
cycles`. A byte either side *wrote* must match; a byte only one side *read* is a
coverage note, because a C dispatch may be coarser than the guest's instruction
(the text routine's port reads its row's tile one instruction early so the atlas
upload happens before the VDP address is computed) and a read that mattered shows
up in the exit state. Two live runs from a fuzzed seed are not held to the
recording's window at all.

The 23 committed fixtures (1.8 MiB) cover the routines the whole-recording checks
already prove -- the controller read, the guest's two text routines, the house
selection, the title screen, the options screen's frame routine, the mission
cursor's placement handler, the map view's transcriptions and the build panel --
so the same C now passes an end-to-end comparison and a per-state one. Seven rows
have none and the check declares each with its reason rather than letting a
missing file look like a pass: six are not reached by the committed recordings
(the mission recording plays the map in placement mode, so the normal cursor's
handler and its step body are never entered; two scroll helpers sit on branches
the routes do not take; the starport's unit-order menu is never opened), and one
is measured but not replayable as a unit -- the options screen's loop, because the
port's C keeps a flag of its own (the loop is active) that is not guest state, so
the dispatch at $01097C answers differently outside the screen's flow. The corpus
found that, which is the point of it.

`tests/test_oracle.sh` (fast tier, ~8 s) runs the corpus's own fixtures (the
file's round trip, the empty set's refusal, the first divergence on every axis,
the recorder on a two-instruction routine in work RAM), every committed fixture
against its C and against the live guest, the declared measured/unmeasured lists,
and four controls: an empty fixture exits 3, a fixture pointed at another
implementation is refused, a fixture from another cartridge is refused by its
SHA-256, and a control build with `ReadController`'s six-button cost off by one
fails and names the cost. `tests/test_oracle_capture.sh` (slow tier, ~2.5 min)
regenerates the set from the three committed recordings with
`scripts/oracle_fixtures.sh` and requires the same bytes, so a fixture cannot
drift on its own. `make -C port test` runs the corpus's own fixtures with the
rest.

Ticket 175's staged recipe now says how its `measured` stage is measured (a
routine with no fixture is not portable yet) and banks the fixtures in its
`paired` stage's proof; the corpus is also the `solo` stage's oracle. The
mechanism, its measurements and what it cannot measure are
`docs/next-engine.md` ("The oracle is the transliterated code"),
`docs/port-architecture.md` ("The state corpus"), D92 and the glossary.
