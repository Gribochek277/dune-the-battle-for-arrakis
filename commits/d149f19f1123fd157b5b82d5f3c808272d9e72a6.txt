D150: the played build pays the 68000 for the driver it no longer runs
The played build runs no Z80, and its state left the reference arrangement's
(next with the Z80 driver) from frame 7 313 of the playtest route, inside 6 of
the 10 banked steps. Replaying the reference's own 513 633 bus charges, at the
machine cycles they were made, and its answers to the 68000's sound-RAM reads
into the played build made the two identical on every frame of the route: the
driver's copies through its bank window (8 cycles of 68000 each) and its answer
to the DMA routines' handshake ($1B20/$1B21) are the whole difference.

next/audio_driver_bus.c is the model of that footprint, installed as the
machine's Z80 stand-in wherever the port's engine is the sound path. The
sequencer tells it, in the driver's order, what the driver would have copied --
a song's 2 + 49 bytes, a voice's 2 + 39, an effect's 2 + 32, a drum's 12-byte
record and its sample's 128-byte halves, a stream's 16-byte window on a miss
-- and the model charges each byte where the driver would have, paced by the
driver's own work between copies (T-states and hook calls per piece, measured
on the reference) and by the DAC hook's Timer A waits while a sample plays. It
keeps the driver's three destination pools as bookkeeping (the reference
pool's helpers, exported as next_audio_pool_*), each channel's voice slot (the
driver keeps the last voice from song to song; the engine loads voice 0 at a
song start) and command 23's mute, which the engine never heard
(next_audio_driver_bus_mute(), from next_sound_command23()); the engine's
audio is unchanged by either. It answers the handshake in sound RAM, so
docs/port-zones.json gives it the $1B20/$1B21 bytes (touchers 249 -> 250,
allowances 47 -> 48).

The playtest route now runs inside 10 of 10 steps, and the picture is the
reference arrangement's on every frame. headless's --no-driver-bus leaves the
model out; it is the control, and it gives the numbers before this commit.

port/Makefile: next/audio_driver_bus.c joins NEXT_SRC, and the audio-engine
harness links it (the engine calls it; it does nothing until installed).
