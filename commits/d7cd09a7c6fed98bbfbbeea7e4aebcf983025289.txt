184: the frame block and the input state are the port's C
Everything above the machine reads two things -- how many frames have passed and
what this frame's buttons are -- and they are one group now: `frame-clock`, solo
with entry `seam` (D94), replacing the `input` group whose one row was the pad
protocol. Twelve rows: `ReadController` (ticket 83, unchanged), the VBlank
handler `$0016CC` one instruction per dispatch, `WaitVBlank`,
`GetFrameCounter`, `SetFrameHandler`, `SetSpriteUploadHandler`,
`MarkFrameBoundary`, `UpdateFrameClock`, `ResetFrameClock`, `FlushPadState`,
`ReadPadDirection` and `UpdateInputState`.

The handler is not a call: the frame clock stacks SR and the interrupted pc and
the handler leaves through the shared rte stub, so a row now says how the guest
enters it (`entry`: `NEXT_ENTRY_CALL`, the default, or `NEXT_ENTRY_INTERRUPT`)
and the dispatch checks that frame -- a call into the vector's target is
refused. The exception frame's contents are not checked and cannot be: the
corpus seeds work RAM from the bytes a dispatch touched, and a handler never
reads its own frame.

Four shapes in one group, each the routine's own. Seven rows are spans because
they wait for a frame or call a row (a whole-routine dispatch cannot wait, and a
call to another row can only be a hand-off); four are whole routines. The whole
routines charge their instructions through `next_replacement_charge()` --
ticket 183's rule -- because on the mission route they are called a million
times (`GetFrameCounter` is 1.1 M) and the seam's clock-step charging moves the
Z80's poll phase.

Measured. The state layer gained the frame block's five fields and the direction
byte (`guest_vblank_counter()` and friends, one bus access at the guest's width
each), and the group's 134 addresses answer at parity: the inventory's
1,730,911,024 guest cycles are its 1,730,911,024 C cycles, zero delegations.

Ten of the twelve rows carry a committed fixture (the playtest route for the
mission's rows, the house selection for `WaitVBlank`'s poll), and each replays
green against the C, the live guest and the fuzz. Two are declared:
`VBlankHandler` cannot be measured -- its fourth instruction,
`bset #8,($A12000).l`, reads the Z80's bus-acknowledge line, which the corpus
does not carry, so a capture of the site diverges on the guest's side too --
and `SetSpriteUploadHandler` is entered by no committed route. Both are proved
from constructed states in `port/tests/test_frame_clock.c`: the guest's own code
and the C run the whole handler from the vector's frame through the rte stub to
the interrupted pc, and the registers, flags, pc, all 64 KiB of work RAM, the
VDP counters and the cycle count are compared.

The pair: 2 700 frames of the house-selection recording, legacy against
`--replace-group frame-clock`, identical in picture, work RAM, chip counters,
audio, YM timeline and the 68000 cycle count, the instruction count lower by
exactly the 65 236 the four whole-routine rows report; the mission route to
entry 7 898 holds the same cycles, picture and work RAM, its audio is the same
recording (528 windows, envelope r +0.999), and its YM stream is 892 259 writes
against `ReadController`'s own 892 261 -- the group is two writes from ticket
83's row, so the new rows do not move the sound. `--no-replace` is legacy byte
for byte. The counters the ticket names -- `FramesElapsed`, both VBlank counters,
`FrameCounter`, the four pad bytes, the mask and `PadDirectionCode` -- are the
guest's at entry 7 898, and a scripted press leaves the same held byte on both
engines. The two mission-clock entries ticket 115 left do not move: 210 against
the reference's 208, the roll 986 + 0/8, so they remain the reference's VDP FIFO
slot scheduling and not this slice's.

Findings. `UpdateInputState`'s first call is the mission's unit command handler
(`$0089AA`), not the pad-to-direction routine pass 3 recorded --
`ReadPadDirection` `$0026A6` is that, and `UpdateInputState` does not call it;
and the pending byte it publishes, `$FFFFBF2A`, is written zero by all five
writers in this image. Ticket 174's "no cap on a selection" was not true of the
front end: three `HEADLESS_REPLACEMENT_MAX` (64) arrays and the window's own
held every enabled name, the table had 63, and this group took it to 74 -- the
default run wrote past the end of a stack array and snapshots refused "the
run's identity is longer than a state's fields". The bound is 256 now and the
header says it must clear the table.

`tests/test_frame_clock.sh` is the check (slow tier); the playtest route's
frame-clock mix runs the whole route on the group alone.
