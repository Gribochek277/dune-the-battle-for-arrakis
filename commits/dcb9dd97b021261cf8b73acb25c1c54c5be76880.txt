ticket 100: the port's screen is clean, and the two defects the owner saw are now tests
Both were mine, and both were invisible to every check I had written:

* The text was black because the palette is 128 bytes at $02A82E and I copied 48.
  The glyph font draws its strokes with colour 8 and its outline with 15; colour 8
  of palette 3 is $0EEE -- white -- and it lives at byte 96, past my copy.
* The pink pixels were worse: an earlier version read the name table with
  md68k_read2() to clear a priority bit. V-RAM is not in the 68000's address space
  -- that is why the reference capture drives the VDP ports at all -- so the read
  returned ROM and the write put it into the name table. Twenty-seven pixels of
  $0E0E, a colour a Mega Drive cannot produce, were the visible half.

The fix is not to clear the priority bit at all. The artwork's rows differ, and the
screen puts its text where the tilemap sets no priority: a layout decision the port
owns, not an edit to an asset it borrowed.

The test now asserts what the eye caught. The plane A name table, the backdrop tiles
and the glyph font must be byte for byte what the game's own options screen has --
a screen of the port's own may add cells and may never damage what it borrowed -- and
palette 3 colour 8 must be $0EEE with white pixels actually on the screen. Full gate:
ALL CHECKS PASSED.
