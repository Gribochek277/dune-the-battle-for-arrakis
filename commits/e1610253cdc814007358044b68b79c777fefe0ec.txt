port: run the game's sound driver on a real Z80
The 68000's Z80 handshake had nothing on the other side of it: $A00000+
was a byte store that only looked like RAM, and the driver's answer could
never come. This vendors a Z80 and puts a machine around it.

The core is superzazu/z80 by Nicolas Allemand (MIT, two dependency-free C99
files, upstream states zexdoc/zexall pass), taken verbatim from commit
d64fe10a2274e5e40019b1086bf7d8990cbc5f23, recorded in tools/REVISIONS.txt
and with its licence, hashes and provenance in port/runtime/z80/README.md.
port/runtime/mdz80.c owns everything around it:

  - the 8 KiB of sound RAM both CPUs see ($A00000-$A01FFF from the 68000,
    $0000-$1FFF from the Z80, mirrored over the lower half of each window
    as BlastEm decodes it), the $A11100 bus request/acknowledge handshake
    (the Z80 acknowledges at its next instruction boundary, bit 0 of the
    byte reads 1 until then, and 68000 accesses made without the bus are
    dropped and counted), and the $A11200 reset line;
  - the Z80's own address space: the YM2612's four byte ports
    ($4000-$4003), the nine-bit bank register ($6000), the VDP window
    ($7F00-$7F0F, routed to the port's VDP stub) and the PSG ($7F11);
    $8000-$FFFF is the 68000 bus at (bank << 15) | offset, read and
    written through the same decode the 68000 uses, with no contention;
  - one Z80 instruction after every 68000 instruction: the port has no
    cycle model for either CPU, so this is the whole timing model;
  - YM2612 and PSG register writes recorded in order as events (a 256-deep
    ring with counters that keep counting), address/data latches shared by
    both CPUs, no synthesis yet;
  - a write hook that offers every 68000 write in $A00000-$A0FFFF in order,
    with whether the handshake applied it: that is the driver-upload trace.

md68k_mem.c now hands the Z80 area over byte by byte (so a word write
reaches the YM2612 as the two port operations it is) and answers $A11100
and $A11200 from the machine's bus state instead of storing them.

The differential harness carries the same Z80: its JSON gains port.z80
(instructions, cycles, pc, sp, interrupt and bus state, bank register, the
YM/PSG counters and the whole of sound RAM), --z80-log writes the upload
trace, and the VBlank it delivers also pulses the Z80's interrupt input.
Frames 1, 2 and 3 still equal the reference byte for byte with the Z80
running, and --frames 60 still stops in the same place: frame 4 in the VDP
DMA wait, which is the 68000 spinning, not the Z80. By that stall the Z80
has run 132 303 instructions and is still in its own command loop.

port/tests/test_z80.c runs the cartridge's own routines rather than a
re-implementation: a hand-assembled program out of sound RAM (YM2612, PSG,
RAM, halt), the handshake and reset semantics, the control registers, the
bank register and its window, the YM2612 events, the upload at $0D5330
traced byte for byte against $08514A-$0869AB (8192 writes: the driver, its
zero terminator and the padding to $A02000), the restart at $0D5374, the
driver's own start-up (PSG silence, voice table, the $2B7 patch, main
loop), its interrupt handler at $0038 and a second VBlank after it, the
$0D52B6 handshake, and the game's sound init at $0D542E -- upload, restart,
command 11 -- whose 14 queued bytes the driver consumes, leaving the game's
12 parameter bytes at $A6C.
