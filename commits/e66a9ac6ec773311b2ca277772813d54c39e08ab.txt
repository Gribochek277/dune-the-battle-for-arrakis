port: the engine keeps the driver's voice slot and obeys command 23's mute
D150's items 2 and 3, the two audio differences it recorded and left to the
audio's owner (ticket 7, D161).

Command 23 (`$0AD9`, `$0D557C`) mutes one channel of a song; the sequence pass
tests the channel's flag bit 1 at `$0512` and dispatches none of its notes. The
port had the field and nobody wrote it: `sequence_channel.flags` documented bits
0, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 7 and left bit 1 blank. `next_audio_engine_mute()` writes it
and `note_on()` -- the one place every note passes -- returns before the effect's
restart and before either allocator, which is `$0512`'s position. The bus model's
second copy of the bit (`bus.muted`) is gone; it charges the command's cost
(`next_audio_driver_bus_command23()`) and nothing else.

A channel's voice is the driver's slot (`$1861 + 39n`), written only by a voice
opcode and filled by the start-up at `$088A`. The primary site was the song
start, not the reset: `next_audio_engine_play()` cleared the channel and then
loaded voice record 0 on every song. It now keeps the 39 bytes and loads no
voice; `next_audio_engine_reset()` starts every slot at the fill, and
`next_audio_engine_reset_channels()` keeps the slots across `$0C04`.

Measured. The driver's own fill, run to its main loop in `port/tests/test_z80.c`,
is `$FF` in the slot's type byte and `$00` in the other 38 -- all sixteen slots,
not two. On the playtest route the briefing sends command 23 once, frame 7548,
song 7, channel 2; the control (`--no-audio-mute`, the new diagnostic, modelled
on `--no-driver-bus`) names the eight drum notes the cartridge silences (7555,
7562, 7570, 7577, 7600, 7622, 7630, 7638, song stops 7644); the played path
dispatches none of them and no other event on the route moves. Against the
cartridge's own driver in that window the residual drops 562 -> 499 RMS.

The slot's finding is that it was latent here: every song of set 0 played through
a HEAD build and this one differs in no note event at all, and the route's
YM2612 timeline is byte-identical -- no song in this cartridge plays a note
before setting its voice. D161 records that rather than claiming an audible fix.

`port/tests/test_audio_engine.c` (area `audio_engine`) proves the mechanism:
the power-on slot, the slot across a song start and a channel reset, and command
23 silencing the channel with the most notes while the other fifteen are
untouched. `tests/test_audio_modern.sh` proves the route's mute and its control.
The audio comparisons needed no re-basing: the reference engine obeys the same
command on the same frame, which is what makes it the driver's model.
