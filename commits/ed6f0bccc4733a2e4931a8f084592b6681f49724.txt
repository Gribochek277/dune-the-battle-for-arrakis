Presentation time: the picture runs at the display's rate, the guest keeps its 59.92
The loop tied one picture to one guest frame. Now a renderer that keeps
snapshots is captured at each VBlank entry and drawn between the entries: with
vsync at the display's own rate, without it at `--present-rate HZ`, and with
`--no-interpolate` the one-picture-per-guest-frame path every existing run knew.
A 120 Hz target shows 120 pictures while the guest runs at 59.92, and the
guest's work RAM, cycles, audio and input are the same to the byte.

The seam grew an optional half rather than a second registry: `capture`,
`draw_at(alpha)` and `cut` on `md_renderer`, with `md_renderer_draw()` untouched
for software, stub and every fidelity comparison, so ticket 121's GPU backend
is three more fields and no loop change. The schedule is the pacing's second
deadline (guest frames and picture slots counted apart), and the alpha is how
far the wall clock is through the guest frame's slot: 0 at the previous
deadline, 256 at the one the loop waits for, and 256 at any cut.

A cut is structural, not motion: the palette, the frame's bits, the plane bases
and size, the hscroll table and its mode, the window and the sprite table snap;
the per-line scroll, V-SRAM, the sprite list and the pattern data blend. A
scene transition is not a signal the VDP has -- it is a change of both plane
bases and the palette, and the same rule covers it.

The fixtures (`port/tests/test_present.c`, `port/tests/test_scene.c`) pin the
schedule and the picture: a midpoint that moves a plane scroll and a sprite by
exactly half a step, endpoints byte-identical to the reference, and each cut
snapping. The window's check (`tests/test_presentation.sh`) measures 300 frames
at 120 pictures a second with byte-identical RAM, VDP state, WAV and pad
timeline against the interpolation-off run, and `--scale 8` dropping pictures
without moving the guest. The report now names guest frames, instructions,
cycles, presented and dropped apart. D42 records the decision.
