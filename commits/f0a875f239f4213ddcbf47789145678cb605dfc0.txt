port: record every work-RAM access with the pc that made it
The understanding phase needs to know where the game keeps its state, and
reading code answers that slowly. The runtime now offers every access whose
first byte is in work RAM to a hook (md68k_set_ram_hook), once per access with
the width and the pc of the instruction that made it, and the headless harness
writes that trace with --ram-log plus a '# frame N' marker at every VBlank
entry, so the accesses of one frame interval can be told from the next.

The interrupt frame the harness pushes is the machine's write, not an
instruction's, and is traced like any other, with the interrupted instruction
as its pc -- the same thing the 68000 would report. The counters under
port.ram_accesses are always reported, logged or not.

scripts/ram_map.py aggregates the trace into analysis/ram-map.json (one record
per address, the pcs and clusters that touch it, the blanket RAM
initialisations) and cross-checks it against two reference captures.
tests/test_ram_map.sh proves the map, its determinism and the cross-check
without the emulator; tests/run_all.sh runs it. The map and the trace are
derived data and git-ignored.
