Name the pad's extra-button bytes for what they are, not a direction
analysis/symbol-names.txt called $FFFFE41C/$FFFFE41D PadDirectionPressed and
PadDirectionHeld -- "a dpad nibble, newly pressed and held". Ticket 83 found the
disagreement in the first C replacement's comments and left the names alone,
because renaming them regenerates the disassembly sources and that is a change
with its own review. This is that change.

What the bytes are. ReadController ($0011C2) runs the pad protocol's seven
steps and keeps the low nibble of its last TH-high read ($00120E -> $001216):
the 6-button pad's *extra* buttons -- Z on bit 0, Y on bit 1, X on bit 2, Mode on
bit 3, 1 = pressed -- held at $FFFFE41D and newly pressed at $FFFFE41C, both
written on the 6-button path only, with the same nibble handed to the caller in
d0's high word ($001214/$00122E swap it in and out). The names are now
PadExtraHeld ($FFFFE41D) and PadExtraPressed ($FFFFE41C).

Why the direction reading looked right, and why it is wrong. The pad's phase
table (chips/pad.h) puts the extra buttons on the third TH rising edge with TH
high, and the reader's last high read follows its *fourth* $40 write -- one edge
too late for the extras, which reads as a dpad. But the pad counts rising edges
and the write count is not the edge count: the guest leaves TH high at the end
of a poll, so the next poll's first $40 write is not an edge, and the poll's
first read resets the counter the 56 000-cycle timeout expired. A poll's edges
are therefore its 3rd, 5th and 7th write ($0011E4, $0011F8, $00120E) and the
read the guest keeps is the extra-button phase. The one poll that reads the dpad
phase there is the first after reset, whose first $40 write *is* an edge --
which is why the cold poll in the port's own fixtures looked like a dpad read.

The evidence, measured against the reference (BlastEm v1.0.0), not argued: with
the extra button X held from VBlank entry 2200, the reference leaves $FFFFE41D =
$04 at entry 2216 and the port's run of the same sequence leaves $04 too; with
the dpad's Right held from the same entry it leaves $00 there with $08 in
PadHeld $FFFFE41B, on both sides. Both runs are checks now
(tests/test_input.sh, `compare_input.py --press 2200:q` / `--press 2200:Right`),
and port/tests/test_pad.c replays the reader's poll against the chip model and
checks the nibble ("the reader's extra-button read") plus the cold-poll case.
