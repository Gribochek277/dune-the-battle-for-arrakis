port: count cycles, price DMA, and clock frames from them
Ticket 55. The frame clock was an 18 000-instruction budget standing in for a
frame; it now counts cycles:

- runtime/md68k_cycles.c charges every instruction the 68000 clocks the M68K
  PRM gives it, cross-checked against BlastEm 1.0.0's translators so the port
  follows the emulator's timeline, and the harness asserts the VDP's VBlank
  every 128 006 cycles (262 scanlines of 3420 master clocks at 7 master clocks
  per 68000 cycle -- BlastEm's own grid). The constants are in
  runtime/timing.h, each with the observation behind it.
- the VDP keeps ticket 43's status semantics but prices the DMA busy window the
  way BlastEm does (16 master clocks per word in H40, 20 in H32, doubled for a
  V-RAM destination or a copy, plus the 12 slots of start-up a 68k -> VDP
  transfer gets), so the game's $000E68 poll waits as long as it does on the
  emulator.
- fixtures: tests/test_cycles.c pins the per-instruction times for the forms
  the game uses, the two execution paths charging the same, the reset stub's
  cycle total (363 792) and both DMA costs.

Measured: compare_port_state.py --frames 100 is identical (all 65 536 bytes of
work RAM and every register); frames 1..823 agree frame by frame; the guest's
frame counter stays in step with the reference to frame 10 000, where the
previous clock had lost it by frame ~900. The first divergence is frame 824 --
the VBlank callback pointer at $FFFFE002 and the VDP command queue at
$FFFFC5A8 -- a phase effect of the estimated cycle counts: this stretch of the
intro does nearly a frame's work per frame, so the residual error decides
whether a boundary catches the emulator inside a routine the port has already
left. Scaling the DMA cost by +-25 % does not move it, which rules the DMA
model out and is why no constant was tuned. The intro's pictures still differ
at frame 1000 (36.86 % of pixels), while the timeline does not.
