PC+index operands read the address the cartridge reads
The owner's screenshot from the third mission: the playfield became squares and
artifacts the moment a sandworm ate a unit, the sprite layer and the HUD intact;
leaving the build screen repaired most of it and left two sprite fragments.

The cause is one part that dropped a displacement. The cartridge has 141
`(d16,PC,Xn)` source operands -- jump and length tables -- and the listing prints
the *resolved* base: the extension word's address plus the brief extension
word's signed displacement. `LoadViewportDescriptor`'s size-table lookup
(`$00528A`) is the one that matters here: `d8(PC,d1.w)` with a displacement of
-80 reads `$00523C`, and the part read `$00528C` instead -- the routine's own
body, whose word `$6712` the DMA queue used as a transfer length. 26 386 words
for a frame whose cell says 256: the worm's animation frame written across V-RAM
from `$DD20`, wrapped through `$0000`, and the map's tile block destroyed.

The part now reads the base the guest reads. `tests/test_pc_indexed_operands.py`
pairs every such instruction with its part by address and requires the body to
name the base (127 of the 138 are in the parts; the rest are still the guest's),
and its `--control` plants the defect back in memory and requires the check to
name it. `tests/vram_watch.py` is the measurement the report needed: two saved
states diffed as V-RAM against the mission's plan. Both are in `run_all.sh`;
docs/native-port.md and docs/reverse-engineering-notes.md record the finding.
