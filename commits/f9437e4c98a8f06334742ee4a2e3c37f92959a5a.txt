166: the mouse's edge-scroll band is the window's border, in window points
The mission's camera scroll tested the gameplay surface's bounds; it now tests
the physical window's. The pointer arrives in window points -- SDL's unit,
before the layout transform -- so the band is read there: within
MDA_POINTER_BAND_POINTS (24) of any window border, bars included. The picture's
scale and placement can no longer move it, a hand over a bar is part of the band
and not a hole in it, and the same window point asks for the same direction at
every world scale. The pad's and the keyboard's band keep the cartridge's own
numbers, and the middle-button drag asks the band for nothing.

The guest's arithmetic is untouched. On a pointer frame the sixteen instructions
D58 owns are still the guest's own compare-branch-subtract, charged at its own
address, with the comparison's operand made from the record and the direction: an
axis inside the band compares the record with itself (the guest's idle pair
runs), a low edge with the record plus one (blt is taken) and a high edge with
the record minus one (bgt is taken). The subtraction hands the guest the old
one-twentieth-of-the-picture distance (16 pixels across, 11 down), so the step
is its one-pixel floor and the remainders end at zero.

Where it lives:
  * app/pointer.{c,h}: mda_pointer_window_band() is the band (the window's
    points; outside the window is no direction) and
    mda_pointer_point_to_picture() is the picture pixel nearest a point over a
    bar, for the cursor's record.
  * app/play_backend.c, app/app.{c,h}, app/input_map.h: the poll's pointer is
    one struct -- valid, moved, inside, band_x/y and the near pixel -- and the
    loop hands it to the front end whole.
  * platform/window.{c,h}: mdp_window_pointer_inside() tracks ENTER/LEAVE and
    FOCUS_GAINED/LOST, because SDL's position is a level it keeps reporting after
    the hand has gone; a driver with no such events (the dummy one) is a window
    the pointer is on, so the scripted checks are unchanged.
  * next/mission_pointer.{c,h}: the frame's window facts ride with its position
    (set_band(), near_x/y, band_x/y); a move anywhere in the window is a pointer
    edge, while a click on a bar is still no gesture.
  * next/mission_mouse.c: pointer_record() is the record (the hand's pixel, or
    the picture's nearest one over a bar); a frame the middle button holds asks
    the band for nothing, so the drag's whole-cell commit is not double-stepped.
  * next/mission_edge_band.{c,h}: the window's direction per axis and the
    record-made operands, with next_mission_edge_band_step_x()/y() naming the
    distance. D82 records the decision.

What proves it:
  * port/tests/test_pointer.c: the band at the corners, edges, interior and
    inner boundary of two window sizes, the constant not being a fraction, the
    outside-window answer, and the nearest picture pixel over a bar.
  * port/tests/test_replacement.c: the pad's band still matches the guest's own
    code (registers, flags, bytes, cycles); a hand in the window's interior is
    the guest's run byte for byte; the window's band walks the camera one pixel a
    frame; a hand over a bar walks it the other way with the record held at the
    picture's edge; and the drag fixture now carries a non-zero band, so it would
    part from the pad's C run if the drag asked the band.
  * tests/test_mission_edge_band.sh: the same on the Atreides recording through
    the playable window, with a bar run (854x480, fit-height: 647 -> 537 over 110
    frames with the record at the picture's edge) and its control.
  * tests/test_mission_band_window.sh (new): a real pointer on a private X
    display (Xvfb and xdotool) at two target sizes and world scales 1x..4x, the
    direction per frame from the port's new --report-band line; the same window
    point asks the same direction at every scale, the bar at 1280x720/4x is the
    band and the picture's first columns are not, and a pointer outside the
    window asks for nothing.

Docs: docs/mouse.md's edge section, docs/next-engine.md's measurements,
