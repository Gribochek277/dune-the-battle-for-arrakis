D143: WASD scrolls the camera and leaves the cursor
The owner's playtest, with a hand on the mouse and the other on `WASD`: pressing
a direction walked the selection box. The cartridge's d-pad has that shape on
purpose -- the pad moves the view, the box settles into the guest's dead zone --
but the port writes the hand's pixel into the guest's cursor record on every
resting frame (tickets 127, 147), and a key that then dragged the box was
fighting the hand. The owner's rule is the device's: with a pointer in the run
`WASD` scrolls the camera and nothing else, the arrows stay the cartridge's
d-pad, and a run with no pointer at all keeps the cartridge's behaviour on
`WASD` too.

- `app/input_map.c` gives `W`/`A`/`S`/`D` a second row (`MDA_FUNCTION_SCROLL`,
  one axis a key in the pad's own bits); `mda_input.scroll` collects it and the
  front end hands it to the mission with the pointer's frame.
- The camera's pace is the PORT SETTINGS `SCROLL` row (SLOW / NORMAL / FAST, 4,
  8, 12 *screen* pixels a guest frame, divided by the map's scale) with
  `--scroll` and the display file's `scroll` key behind it: one camera, one
  pace, whether the hand rests in the border or a key is held.
- The row is saved like the display's values; `app/state_file.c` carries it
  through a loaded state and refuses a stale one.

The tests: `tests/test_mission_key_scroll.sh` holds a real `X` on the Atreides
mission and requires the camera to move by the pace while the cursor's record
`$FFFFBF12` does not move, with the arrow key and `--no-mouse` as the
cartridge's own control and `--scroll slow/fast` as the row's;
`port/tests/test_input_map.c`, `test_settings_screen.c` and
`test_replacement_rows.c` hold the second row, the settings screen's new row and
the frame with the guest; `test_display_*` and `test_port_settings_screen.sh`
read the row's words out of the VDP. `run_all.sh` runs them.

docs: D143 in docs/decisions.md, the row in docs/display.md, the keys in
docs/keyboard.md and docs/mouse.md, the hand's row in docs/next-engine.md, and
docs/port-architecture.md's key paragraph.
